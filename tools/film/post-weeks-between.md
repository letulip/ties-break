## Main post

**Title:** The weeks between the tournaments

Most of a junior tennis career is not played on court. It is Tuesday.

Ties Break: Ace Parent is a career sim where you play the parent – the one booking the courts,
paying for them, and driving there. The tournaments matter. But the game lives in the weeks between
them, and this clip is about what those weeks actually look like.

**Every week crosses itself out.**

When you send her into a week, the calendar strikes through it a day at a time. Monday, Tuesday,
Wednesday. You can skip it with a tap. I would rather you didn't – watching a week get crossed off
is the most honest thing the game does. That is the real resource. Not money. Weeks.

**And no two weeks have the same shape.**

**Training week.** School from eight to one, a session in the afternoon, homework in the evening.
The default, and the backbone of the season. A dozen of these in a row and you can feel her getting
better and feel yourself getting tired.

**Exams.** For two weeks of the season the school blocks break apart into scattered papers – Monday
morning, Tuesday afternoon, Friday before lunch. No tournaments that week. Her five sessions still
stand, because you were the one who scheduled them, and the game will not quietly cancel them on
your behalf. The note on the fridge reads: *"Left you a snack for after the exam."*

**Summer block.** Nine weeks with no school at all – an early hit at nine and the afternoon still
free, so, as the screen puts it, "there is room to double up". This is where the biggest jumps come
from. It is also how you break a fourteen-year-old, and the game keeps that door open on purpose.
*"Recycling goes out in the morning."*

**Family week.** You book this one, and you pay for it. A seaside family hotel runs several hundred
– the quote moves with the week – and a staycation with friends is free. The whole timetable turns over – travel out, a lie-in,
the sea, the pool, the last day, the flight home. No sessions. No tournaments. She comes back
measurably stronger, which is the joke and also the entire point.
*"Sandwiches are in the blue box."*

There are more shapes than fit in half a minute: an off-season, a tournament week, and the one
nobody wants – the week she is on the bench, where the grid is empty and there is nothing for you to
plan at all.

None of this is decoration. An exam fortnight is a hard constraint on your season plan. A summer
block is an opportunity you can absolutely waste. A holiday is a line item you have to justify
against the coach you wanted to hire instead.

You are not managing a player. You are managing a childhood, and there is a fixed number of weeks
in it.
