# What a player's own brand is worth – RF, and four brands that were not racquet deals

**The research round 30 #9 ordered, written BEFORE the number was picked.** The owner's order,
verbatim:

> «сам Merch brand тоже вполне может расти в цене как бизнес по какой-то логике, похожей на привязку
> к её рекламе и результатам. Можно провести анализ доходов и стоимости бренда RF (Roger Federer) для
> референса»

**Every number below is tagged.** `[S]` = stated in the cited source, read here rather than quoted
from a summary of it. `[I]` = inferred or computed here from sourced inputs, with the arithmetic
shown. `[GAP]` = looked for and not found; a stated gap is worth more than a plausible invention.
`[WEAK]` = a figure that circulates but whose trail ends at an aggregator or an unread snippet.

This page EXTENDS [off-court-money.md](off-court-money.md) (the census: off-court money is not
ordered by ranking) and [endorsement-tiers-and-academy-money.md](endorsement-tiers-and-academy-money.md)
(the tier table, and the academy's own accounts). Those two priced what a brand PAYS a player. This
one prices the brand ITSELF – a different question, and the answer turned out to be a different
shape.

---

> **THE FOUR ANSWERS, IN ONE PARAGRAPH.**
> **(1) HIS OWN REFERENCE HAS NO PUBLISHED VALUE, and that is the finding rather than a failure of
> searching.** The RF mark sits in Tenro AG, Federer's private Swiss holding company, which files no
> accounts; On Holding's own IPO prospectus and its FY2025 annual report both name Tenro **only** in a
> risk factor and never in the financials, so not even the royalty is public. The two figures in
> circulation – $27M and a rule-of-thumb $6.75M – are a lawyer's aside and an illustration `[WEAK]`.
> **A model that depends on the RF mark's value being knowable cannot be built.**
> **(2) BUT THE INCOME ANSWER IS UNAMBIGUOUS AND IT IS THE OPPOSITE OF WHAT A DECAY WOULD PREDICT.**
> Federer's off-court earnings went **$90M → $95M in the year he retired**, and he was the
> highest-paid tennis player in the world for a 17th consecutive year having not played a match for
> fourteen months. Nothing about a player's brand tracks their current results.
> **(3) THE BEST-DOCUMENTED PLAYER BRAND EQUITY FALLS, HARD, FOR REASONS THE PLAYER CANNOT TOUCH.**
> Federer's On stake `[I]` ran ~$330M at the IPO, ~$145M at the 2022 trough, ~$603M at the January
> 2025 peak and ~$289M in August 2026 – **down about half in nineteen months while he was retired and
> nothing whatsoever about him changed.**
> **(4) AND THE FAILURE MODE OF A NAME-ATTACHED BRAND IS NOT A CRASH, IT IS STARVATION.** Sugarpova,
> S by Serena, EleVen and TB12 all died quietly within about eighteen months of the athlete's last
> season – no bankruptcy, no scandal, no announcement, no sale price. **Survival correlates with
> exactly one thing: whether the asset detached from the person.**

## 1. THE RF MARK – what is knowable, and what is not

| fact | source |
| --- | --- |
| The RF monogram was created in 2008, first used 2010, **registered by Nike and not by Federer** `[S]` | The Fashion Law, «A Look at the Making, Marketing of the Famous Federer "RF" Logo», 29.01.2021 |
| USPTO serial 77443332, registration 3838371, filed 08.04.2008, apparel `[S]` (indexed record; the registry itself refused automated access) | USPTO via Justia |
| He left Nike for Uniqlo in March 2018 and **could not take the mark** – he played two seasons without his own logo `[S]` | EU IP Helpdesk (European Commission), 11.10.2020 |
| **February 2020: Nike assigned the registrations to Tenro AG**, his Swiss holding company (registered 2007, UID CHE-113.912.589) `[S]`. Terms never disclosed `[GAP]` | SportsPro, 12.03.2020; The Fashion Law, 29.01.2021 |
| **On does not own THE ROGER brand.** Its IPO prospectus risk factors state it depends on a licence from Tenro AG for the marks and for his name, image and likeness `[S]` | On Holding AG, Form 424B4, 16.09.2021, p.47 – read directly |
| The identical risk factor still stands four and a half years later `[S]` | On Holding AG, Form 20-F FY2025, filed 03.03.2026 – read directly |
| What On pays Tenro `[GAP]` – Tenro appears in the risk factor and **nowhere in the financials or the related-party notes** of either filing | as above |
| A valuation of the RF mark `[GAP]`. The circulating $27M is a solicitor's remark in a commentary piece and the ~$6.75M is Brand Finance's 20–25%-of-company rule of thumb applied illustratively `[WEAK]` | The Fashion Law, 29.01.2021 |

⭐⭐ **THE STRUCTURE IS THE FINDING, NOT THE NUMBER.** The mark is not inside the operating business.
It sits in a separate holding company and is **licensed out** – to Uniqlo for the apparel line, to On
for the shoe. That is a third option beside «keep it» and «sell it», and it is the one the best
off-court career in the sport actually chose.

⚠ **AND HERE IS THE FACT THAT SHOULD STOP ANY MODEL THAT PRICES A CAREER OFF ITS MARK.** For the two
years 2018–2020 Federer had **lost legal control of his own logo** – and 2020 was the best earning
year of his life ($106.3M, top of the Forbes list, the first tennis player ever to lead it in the
list's thirty-year history `[S]`, ESPN 29.05.2020). Losing the trademark cost him nothing measurable.
**The mark was not where the value lived.**

## 2. WHAT IT EARNED, AND WHAT RETIREMENT DID TO IT

Forbes' annual highest-paid-athletes lists, trailing twelve months, before tax and agents' fees.

| Forbes year | rank | total | on court | **off court** |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2020 | **1** | $106.3M | $6.3M | **$100M** |
| 2021 | 7 | $90M | $0.03M | **$90M** |
| 2022 | 7 | $90.7M | $0.7M | **$90M** |
| 2023 | 9 | $95.1M | $0.1M | **$95M** |
| 2024–2026 | – | absent from the list | | |

The series is Wikipedia's tabulation of the Forbes lists `[S]`, cross-checked independently at both
ends: 2020 at ESPN (29.05.2020) `[S]`, and 2023 at Tennis Majors (03.05.2023) `[S]`, which states the
$0.1M / $95M split, the 1 May 2022 – 1 May 2023 window, and that **he led every athlete on the list in
off-court income**.

⭐⭐⭐ **READ THE 2023 ROW AGAINST THE DATE HE STOPPED.** He retired in September 2022, which is inside
that window. **His off-court income went up $5M in the year he retired** `[S]`. And in 2022 he was the
highest-paid tennis player in the world for the **seventeenth consecutive year** having not played a
match for nearly fourteen months `[S]` (Forbes via Flashscore).

⚠ **HIS ABSENCE FROM THE 2024–2026 LISTS IS NOT AN EARNINGS COLLAPSE** `[I]`. The lists are built on a
May-to-May window and drop athletes who are no longer competing. Forbes still describes him as
collecting an estimated **$90M a year when he hung up his racket** `[S]` (Sportico, 2024, whose tennis
top ten fell to $246M from a record $343M in 2020, «when the now-retired Federer and Serena Williams
accounted for $142 million between them»). No post-2023 annual figure has been published `[GAP]`.

**The contractual mechanism.** The Uniqlo deal signed July 2018 is **$300M over ten years and contains
no retirement clause** – he is paid whether or not he plays `[S]` (Financial Times / Irish Times,
Murad Ahmed, 06.07.2018; the NYT of 08.09.2018 confirms the terms but not the clause).

**Counterparty risk is real and it did fire.** Credit Suisse, a sponsor of 167 years' standing,
collapsed into UBS in March 2023; UBS honoured the inherited deal through at least the end of 2025
`[S]` (Bloomberg and SWI swissinfo.ch, 31.10.2023). **The income stream's counterparty can die while
the athlete is perfectly fine.**

**Where it ended up.** Forbes declared him a billionaire at **$1.1bn** on 22.08.2025 – career prize
money of nearly **$131M** against roughly **$1bn** collected off court `[S]` (Forbes, Justin Birnbaum;
read in the Forbes Africa syndication, forbes.com refusing automated access).

## 3. ON HOLDING – the one player-brand equity that is marked to market, and it moves BOTH ways

He took **shares instead of a cheque** in November 2019, described in On's own filings as a
«co-entrepreneur» `[S]` (Form 424B4, 16.09.2021).

⚠⚠ **THE STAKE HAS NEVER BEEN DISCLOSED, AND THIS WAS CHECKED IN THE PRIMARY DOCUMENT RATHER THAN
ASSUMED.** The prospectus's beneficial-ownership table lists the three founders, two executives and
the 5%-plus holders (Point Break 25.4%, Stripes 12.4%, Hillhouse 6.1%). **Neither Federer nor Tenro AG
appears anywhere in it** `[S]` – the position sits below the 5% disclosure threshold. **Every «3%» in
circulation is a journalist's estimate** `[GAP]`, and so is the «$50M invested in 2019» `[WEAK]`.

| --- | ---: | --- |

| 15.09.2021 close | ~$35 | +46% on day one; On at ~$11bn `[S]` |
| **11.10.2022** | **$15.44** | **all-time low – 36% BELOW the IPO price** `[S]` |
| **30.01.2025** | **$64.05** | all-time high `[S]` |

| 28.08.2026 | **$28.85** | market cap $9.64bn; −36% over 52 weeks; **−55% from the peak** `[S]` |

`[I]` **The stake's trajectory at an assumed 3% of market capitalisation** – the 3% is itself an
estimate, so read the SHAPE and not the levels:

| moment | `[I]` stake |
| --- | ---: |
| IPO day close | ~$330M |
| October 2022 trough | **~$145M** (−56%) |
| January 2025 peak | **~$603M** (+316%) |
| August 2026 | **~$289M** (−52% from the peak) |

⭐⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE CITATION FOR «IT MUST BE ABLE TO FALL».** Between January 2025 and August 2026 that
asset lost roughly half its value – on the order of $300M – **while nothing whatsoever changed about
Roger Federer.** He had been retired for over two years. He won nothing and lost nothing. It fell
because a shoe company missed a quarter.

⚠ **AND THE TWO BEST SOURCES DISAGREE BY A THIRD.** Forbes put the stake above **$375M** and Bloomberg
at **at least $500M** `[S]`/`[WEAK]`, weeks apart, valuing the same listed asset. Even a marked-to-market
player brand is not a number anyone agrees on.

**For calibration, the business under it** `[S]` (424B4 2021 and 20-F FY2025): net sales CHF 425.3M
(2020) → CHF 2,318.3M (2024) → **CHF 3,014.0M (2025), +30.0%**. `[I]` 7.1x in five years. **The
business compounded hard and the equity did not compound smoothly beside it.**

## 4. FOUR MORE, AND NONE OF THEM IS A RACQUET DEAL

### 4a. Sugarpova – Sharapova's sweets, and the closest analogue in the sport

- Founded **2012** with **$500,000 of her own money, 100% owned** `[S]` (Forbes, 22.01.2016).
- **250,000 bags in the first three months; 5 million bags in 2015 at $4.99; 30 countries by January
  2016** `[S]` (same).
- ⚠ **THERE IS NO REVENUE FIGURE** `[GAP]`. The «$20M» in circulation is a 2016 *goal* in one Forbes
  piece and a 2017 *valuation guess* in another; the «$25M» is `[I]` 5M x $4.99 = $24.95M, which is
  retail sell-through and not company revenue. A «$187M valuation» could not be traced at all `[WEAK]`.
- ⭐⭐ **THE 2016 DOPING BAN DID NOT DENT IT – IT GREW THROUGH IT** `[S]` (Forbes, 10.04.2017): the
  Kroger, 7-Eleven, Hudson and SBE distribution wins were all announced during or after the fifteen-
  month suspension. Her Forbes RANKING fell from first for eleven straight years to eighth. **Her
  endorsement income fell while her owned brand expanded. The two decoupled.**
- **She retired in February 2020 and it died silently.** Accounts quiet, site offline, products off the
  shelves, last post October 2021 `[S]` (Women's Tennis Blog, 10.09.2025). The marks appear to have
  lapsed by 2024 (registry access refused, so `[WEAK]`). **No announcement, no acquirer, no exit price
  – there is no evidence it was ever sold** `[GAP]`.

### 4b. Serena Williams – the line died, the fund lived, and then she took her name off the fund

- **S by Serena** (2018) shut on **31.01.2024** `[S]`; the earlier **Aneres** line (2004) vanished with
  no announcement `[GAP]`. No revenue or valuation was ever published for either `[GAP]`. She retired
  in September 2022 – the line closed about sixteen months later.
- **Serena Ventures** raised a **$111M** debut institutional fund (announced 01–02.03.2022; ~60
  investments, 6 exits, 13 unicorns at launch) `[S]` (Front Office Sports, 03.03.2022). Fund II, AUM
  and returns `[GAP]`.
- ⭐⭐⭐ **JULY 2026: SERENA VENTURES RENAMED ITSELF STARFIRE VENTURES** – 16 unicorns, 5 exits, ~100
  startups `[S]` (Fast Company, 31.07.2026). Her stated reason is the single best line in this
  research: she wanted it to be bigger than her, because *«when you put a name on something, it can
  limit what you do»*. **She stripped her own name off her best-performing asset, deliberately.**

### 4c. Venus Williams – EleVen, and a brand that outlived thirteen years of decline

- Launched **November 2007**, exclusively at Steve & Barry's – **which went bankrupt in 2008 and was
  liquidated, destroying the brand's entire distribution channel in its first year** `[S]`. It had to
  be relaunched independently.
- It then **survived thirteen years of her results declining** after the 2011 Sjögren's diagnosis.
- **February 2024: «pausing operations», with an 80%-off sitewide liquidation** `[S]` (Tennis.com,
  09.02.2024). The domain now 404s. No revenue figure was ever published `[GAP]`.

### 4d. Björn Borg – ⭐⭐⭐ the whole arc, and the one to build the model on

- Retired 1983; launched the Björn Borg Design Group ~1984; **it collapsed in 1989 and declared
  bankruptcy in 1990**. By 1996 eleven creditors owed over $1M between them were pursuing him, he lost
  three court battles over personal liability, and his fortune was largely wiped out – he had refused
  outside financing for fear of losing control `[S]` (Korn Ferry, 24.05.2024; Washington Post,
  25.07.1989).
- **Then the brand detached from him and thrived.** World Brand Management licensed the trademark in
  1997 and **bought it outright at the end of 2006 for $18 million**, listed on Nasdaq Stockholm in
  2007 and renamed itself Björn Borg AB in 2017. **Net sales SEK 1,044M in 2025** (SEK 990M in 2024,
  `[I]` +5.5%; SEK 705.2M in 2020, `[I]` +48% over five years) `[S]` (Björn Borg AB Annual Report
  2025, 27.04.2026).
- **The athlete's own company went bankrupt. The name sold for $18M. The name is now a listed company
  he owns none of.**

### 4e. And the falls, because a value that only rises is not a value

| case | what happened | source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tom Brady, FTX equity** | **$30M to zero**, instantly, blameless (Bündchen $18M) | ESPN, 2023 `[S]` |
| **Shaquille O'Neal, Astrals** | a settlement of **up to $11M** – an athlete brand with **negative** value | Decrypt / DL News, Nov 2024 `[S]` |
| **Jordan Brand** | **FY2025 revenue $7.3bn, DOWN 16%** – `[I]` a ~$1.4bn single-year fall, twenty-two years after he last played, after doubling 2020–24 | Sportico, 27.06.2025 `[S]` |
| **TB12** | Boston store closed 2023, merged into Nobull Jan 2024, **wound down entirely 15.01.2026** | Boston.com, 15.01.2026 `[S]` |
| **Naomi Osaka** | off-court ~$60M (2021) → $51.1M (2022) → **$12.0M (2024)**, `[I]` **−75% in three years, with essentially no sponsors lost** – the fall is playing time | Forbes, 22.12.2022 and 18.12.2024 `[S]` |

...and the two that paid, for the other end of the band: **Kobe Bryant's BodyArmor** – $6M for >10% in
2014, **~$400M to his estate** when Coca-Cola bought it for $5.6bn in November 2021 `[S]` (Forbes,
05.11.2021), `[I]` **66.7x**, and **he had been dead twenty-one months when it paid**; and **David
Beckham's DB Ventures** – Authentic Brands bought **55% for ~$269M in February 2022** `[S]` (CNBC,
24.02.2022), `[I]` implying ~$489M for the whole, with **FY2024 revenue $92.3M and profit $44.9M, up
44%** `[S]` (Fortune, 03.10.2025), eleven years after he retired.

## 5. THE FIVE FINDINGS THE GAME NEEDS

**1. ⭐⭐ BRAND VALUE FOLLOWS THE ACCUMULATED STOCK, NOT CURRENT FORM.** Sugarpova expanded through a
doping ban; EleVen survived thirteen years of decline; Federer earned $90M in a year he played
fourteen months of nothing; Jordan Brand did `[I]` ~$8.7bn twenty-one years after he retired. **The
game's existing decaying fame stock is the right driver and current rank should carry no weight at
all** – which is what `world/fame.ts` already does and what round 30 #13 measured.

**2. ⭐⭐ IT SURVIVES RETIREMENT AS INCOME AND DIES AS A BRAND, AND THE LAG IS STRIKINGLY CONSISTENT.**
Sharapova Feb 2020 → Sugarpova dark Oct 2021 (~20 months). Serena Sept 2022 → S by Serena closed Jan
2024 (~16 months). Brady Feb 2023 → TB12 wound down Jan 2026 (~35). `[I]` **About eighteen months of
grace after the last competitive season, then decay** is defensible from the evidence.

**3. ⭐⭐⭐ IT FALLS, IN SIX DOCUMENTED WAYS**, and only one of them is a crash:
   (a) **silent starvation** – the dominant mode, and it has no terminal event at all;
   (b) **mark-to-market** – On, `[I]` −52% with the athlete unchanged;
   (c) **counterparty failure** – FTX to zero;
   (d) **negative value** – an $11M settlement;
   (e) **category cycle** – Jordan Brand −16%;
   (f) **channel death** – EleVen's retailer went bankrupt in year one.

**4. ⚠ THE MULTIPLE, WHICH IS THE ONE NUMBER A VALUATION NEEDS AND THE ONE THE SPORT WILL NOT GIVE.**
No player-brand transaction publishes both an earnings figure and a price. The two that come closest:
Beckham's DRJB at `[I]` ~$489M implied against FY2024 profit of $44.9M is **~10.9x profit / ~5.3x
revenue**; Nadal's academy at ~€209M against €6.8M net profit and €40M revenue is `[I]` **~31x profit
/ ~5.2x revenue** (from
[endorsement-tiers-and-academy-money.md](endorsement-tiers-and-academy-money.md) §3a). **A single-digit
multiple of what the thing actually earns is the conservative end of a very wide, very thin band**, and
any figure inside it is a choice rather than a citation. `[GAP]` for anything narrower.

**5. ⭐⭐⭐ SURVIVAL CORRELATES WITH EXACTLY ONE THING: WHETHER THE ASSET DETACHED FROM THE PERSON.**
Detached and thrived – Björn Borg (sold for $18M, now a listed company at SEK 1,044M), Jordan Brand
(inside Nike), Beckham's DRJB (55% sold, +44%), BodyArmor (sold, paying a dead man's estate $400M).
Stayed attached and died – Sugarpova, S by Serena, EleVen, TB12. Federer took the third road and
**licensed** it. Serena said the rule out loud in 2026 when she took her own name off her best asset.
