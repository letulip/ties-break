# Live text coverage in junior and youth tennis

Scope: everything below the senior professional tour – ITF World Tennis Tour Juniors (J30–J500),
the Junior Grand Slams, Tennis Europe Junior Tour (U12/U14/U16), ITF junior team competitions,
national federation junior circuits, and US college tennis (NCAA/ITA).

The owner's question was: **is there a principled difference between how junior, youth and adult
tournaments are covered in text?** The answer is yes, and the difference is much sharper and much
more mechanical than "juniors just get less attention".

---

## 0. The one-paragraph answer

There is no live *text commentary* in junior tennis at any level, anywhere, including the Junior
Grand Slams. What varies down the ladder is something narrower: whether a **score feed exists at
all**. That feed exists if and only if a **chair umpire is sitting on the court with a scoring
device**. Junior regulations put a chair umpire in the chair only from the semi-finals or finals
at most levels, so for the overwhelming majority of junior matches nobody is recording the score
in real time – it exists only in the two players' heads until one of them reports the result at
the desk. Everything else (no journalists, no Hawk-Eye, no broadcast) follows from that same
economic floor. Safeguarding law restricts **images and recordings** of minors, but explicitly
**does not** restrict publishing their names, ages, nationalities and full result histories – the
opposite is true, publication of those is mandated by the regulations the players sign.

---

## 1. What coverage actually exists, rung by rung

### 1.1 ITF World Tennis Tour Juniors (J30 → J500)

Six levels: J30, J60, J100, J200, J300, J500, plus the Junior Grand Slams above them. Roughly 980
tournaments in 130 nations.

**What the ITF publishes for every tournament, at every level:** an entry/acceptance list, draws,
order of play, and results. The tournament pages on itftennis.com carry exactly four tabs –
*Draws and Results*, *Order of Play*, *Media And Resources*, *Champions*. Verified on the J500
Milan (Trofeo Bonfiglio) 2026 page, i.e. the second-highest rung of the whole junior ladder.
**There is no "Live Scores" tab even at J500.**

**Live scoring** is governed by the 2026 Organisational Requirements, and the wording is a ladder,
not a switch:

| Level | Live scoring rule (2026) |
|---|---|
| J30, J60, J100, J200 | encouraged, nothing more |
| J300 | recommended for all main-draw matches; required on any court that is being streamed |
| J500 | required "for all matches where there is a Chair Umpire" (ITF Organisational Requirements); required on all streamed courts |

The J500 rule is the key sentence in this whole document. Live scoring is not tied to the
tournament, or the round, or the court – it is tied to **the presence of a human being in the
chair**. No chair, no data.

**Live streaming** (2026, new): recommended on ≥2 courts at J300/J500; **mandatory at J500 on 2
courts from the quarter-finals onwards**; recommended at J300 from first round of main draw. Any
stream must carry an on-screen graphic with the players' names. The ITF's own preview of J500
Milan tells fans matches from the quarter-finals on can be "watched live through the ITF's junior
tennis livestreaming platform" (ITF news) – video.worldtennistourjuniors.com. Note what the ITF
does *not* tell fans: there is no live score centre to point them at.

**Who operates the scoring:** ITF Juniors runs on Tournament Planner / tournamentsoftware.com
(itfjuniors.tournamentsoftware.com). That is draw-and-result administration software operated by
the Supervisor, not a live-scoring pipeline. Sportradar operates the ITF's live-scoring app, but
the *World Tennis Tour Live* app's own App Store listing describes coverage of the professional
entry and mid-levels only, with no mention of juniors anywhere in its feature list.

### 1.2 The Junior Grand Slams

Junior events at the four Slams are part of the ITF junior circuit (they sit above J500 in the
points table) but are staged inside a professional Grand Slam. That changes everything about the
infrastructure without changing anything about the editorial treatment:

- Every match court at a Slam has a chair umpire and an official scoring device, so the score feed
  exists automatically for junior matches. Junior draws and live scores appear on the Slams' own
  sites and are picked up by every aggregator (Sofascore, Flashscore/Livesport, LiveScore).
- The BBC streams every match from all 18 courts at Wimbledon, which structurally includes junior
  matches played on those courts.
- Written coverage exists but is **retrospective**: previews, recaps, and finals reports on
  itftennis.com and the Slam sites, plus BBC / national-press stories when a home player wins.
  I found **no evidence of a ball-by-ball live text feed for any junior Grand Slam match**,
  including finals.

So a junior Grand Slam final gets: live score, live stream, and a written report afterwards. It
does not get a live blog. A J100 first round gets a draw sheet that updates when the result is
typed in.

### 1.3 Tennis Europe Junior Tour (U16, U14) and the U12 events

This is the most interesting rung because it is **changing right now**.

Historic and still-current baseline (2026 Regulations):

- Chair umpires are **mandatory for all singles and doubles main-draw matches only in the
  14/16&U Super Category**. At 14/16&U Categories 1, 2, 3 and at 12&U Categories 1 and 2 a chair
  umpire is mandatory only for **singles and doubles semi-finals and finals**. Everything else is
  played with roving off-court umpires at a recommended ratio of **one umpire per four courts**.
- The regulations contain a whole appendix (Appendix G) on *Matches played without a Chair
  Umpire*: players call their own lines, the server calls the score aloud, ball marks are checked
  by the players themselves on clay.
- Results reach the public through the Supervisor pushing them from the European Junior Tournament
  Planner (EurTP) to the Tennis Europe web system as results are obtained, several times a day
  (Appendix H). That is **batch result publication, not live scoring**.
- Tennis Europe is the exclusive owner of live scoring and live streaming rights; an organiser who
  wants to live score or live stream must ask Tennis Europe in writing in advance and may be made
  to sign an agreement.

**The change:** on 11 May 2026 Tennis Europe named **IONSport** its Official Live Scoring and Data
Partner, covering roughly **540 tournaments and team events, 300+ venues, 45 nations**, rolling out
from **summer 2026**. IONSport's own product description advertises live scoring with real-time
point-by-point tracking and, critically, two capture modes: **Chair Umpire Mode** and **Verified
Crowd Scoring Mode** – i.e. an explicit, sanctioned mechanism for producing a live feed on courts
that have no official in the chair. Tennis Europe's president framed it in development terms:
"Technology plays an increasingly important role in sport development and visibility"
(Henrik Thorsøe Pedersen). This is the first time a junior-tour-wide live data layer has been
attempted at this scale.

**U12 specifically:** Tennis Europe 12&U events are **unranked and unseeded**, and 12&U "starting
points" are explicitly **not published** during the year. The regulations further note that
rankings for players who only play 12&U events are not permitted. So at U12 the governing body
deliberately suppresses the *ranking* layer while still publishing draws and results. That is the
one place where a genuine child-protection-flavoured publication restriction is visible in the
rules – and it is about rankings, not about names or scores.

### 1.4 ITF junior team competitions (14U World Junior Tennis; 16U Junior BJK Cup / Junior Davis Cup)

Team events, 14&U and 16&U, regional qualifying → finals (14U finals at Prostějov, Czechia).
The 2026 Junior Team Competitions Regulations:

- Officiating requirements are not fixed in the rulebook at all; they are "shared by the ITF in
  advance of each stage/event". In other words the chair-umpire coverage is decided per event, and
  at regional qualifying it is thin.
- The same **Appendix F Data Rights** clause appears as in the individual tour: ITF holds exclusive
  live scoring rights.
- Abbreviated formats are allowed in some situations: two **short sets to 4 games** plus a match
  tie-break, No-Ad, No-Let.
- ITF publishes draws and results (per-rubber scores) plus news articles on the finals. The
  tournament page for the 2026 14U Finals shows *Draws and Results*, *Media And Resources*,
  *Player entries*, *Champions* – again, no live scores tab.

### 1.5 National federation junior circuits

**Great Britain (LTA).** Junior County Tour (Grade 4), Junior Regional Tour (Grade 3), and the
Lexus Junior National Championships at 18U/16U/14U/12U/11U. Draws, scores and results for all
levels live on competitions.lta.org.uk and are linked from the LTA news pages. The Junior National
Championships additionally get an LTA-hosted **live stream** from named courts (e.g. "Court 3") and
a written preview article. Below the nationals: draw sheets that update, no stream, no commentary.
This is the cleanest example of the general shape – *a national federation's junior pyramid is fully
published as data and fully unpublished as narrative, with one televised-ish exception at the top.*

**United States (USTA).** Junior tournaments run through TennisLink and its successor Serve Tennis
(playtennis.usta.com). Draws, schedules, results, rankings and player records are all public. I
**could not verify** any official USTA live-scoring product for junior tournaments – the USTA app
listings describe finding tournaments, ratings, rankings and results, not live scores. The live
layer in US juniors is supplied by third parties instead (see 1.7).

**Australia (Tennis Australia).** Junior tournaments run on tournamentsoftware.com
(tournaments.tennis.com.au) plus a Match Centre. Tennis Australia and Tennis Queensland are listed
as customers by **ScoreBuzzer**, a live-scoring vendor whose product explicitly supports "chair
umpires, roving umpires or player self-scoring" – again, self-scoring as a first-class mode.

**Germany (DTB).** Junior tournaments, entries, draws, result tables and LK/ranking calculation all
run through mybigpoint / tennis.de. Result-level publication; I found no evidence of a live-scoring
layer.

### 1.6 US college tennis (NCAA / ITA)

Better covered than junior tennis, and the reason is instructive: college tennis has *institutions
with media departments and a fan base*, so the coverage bought itself.

- The ITA publishes a **weekly listing of live scores and live streams for dual matches, as
  submitted by member teams** – i.e. coverage is opt-in, team-by-team, not universal.
- **Match Tennis App** was named an Official Live Scoring Provider of the ITA (announced Dec 2025),
  with final scores flowing to the ITA for rankings. **Tennis-Ticker** is a preferred provider and
  supplies point-by-point live scoring at up to 10 ITA national-level events.
- **IONSport** claims 1,600+ college programs and lists the Intercollegiate Tennis Association as a
  client.
- **Cracked Racquets** produces YouTube broadcasts of ITA National Fall Championships and
  conference "CrossCourt Casts" – actual commentated video, which junior tennis does not have.
- Format is different too: dual matches are 3 doubles (8-game pro set) + 6 singles, No-Ad, first
  team to 4 points. The unit that matters is the **team score out of 7**, so the natural live
  artefact is a team scoreboard with six court states, not a point stream.

The ITA's own coaching resource states the situation plainly: ESPN, Tennis Channel and Fox Sports
are out of reach for the great majority of college teams; streaming, live scoring and social media
are what is actually within reach.

### 1.7 The third-party layer that actually serves junior families

Not federation products. These are the apps parents actually use:

| Product | Who it serves | Capture mode |
|---|---|---|
| IONSport | Tennis Europe (official, from summer 2026), USTA search, colleges | Chair Umpire Mode **or Verified Crowd Scoring** |
| Match Tennis App | USTA, ITF, ITA, WTN tournaments | user-submitted scores; enhanced live scoring behind a paid tier (Pro ~$12.99/mo) |
| ScoreBuzzer | Tennis Australia, Tennis Queensland, ITA, several national associations; claims ITF Juniors | chair umpire, roving umpire **or player self-scoring** |

All three share one design decision: **the scorer is whoever is standing there.** That is the
structural signature of youth sport data.

## 2. The principled differences – why coverage differs

### 2.1 The chair-umpire gate (the actual root cause)

This is the mechanism everything else hangs off. From the ITF WTTJ 2026 Organisational
Requirements, the designated chair-umpire ladder:

| Level | Chair umpire required from | Off-court umpires |
|---|---|---|
| J30 / J60 / J100 | **the Final only** (national chair umpire) | 1 per 4 courts |
| J200 | mandatory for each Final; recommended from semi-finals | 1 per 3 courts |
| J300 | recommended from quarter-finals, **required from semi-finals**; White Badge mandatory for the Final | 1 per 3 courts |
| J500 | **required from the Round of 32**, recommended from start of main draw; White Badge mandatory from semi-finals | 1 per 2 courts |

Tennis Europe is the same shape one rung lower: chair umpires for all main-draw matches only in the
Super Category, otherwise semi-finals and finals only, with 1 roving umpire per 4 courts.

Consequences that matter for a simulation:

1. A first-round match at a J30 has **no official record of the score while it is happening**. There
   is nothing to publish, and no one is withholding anything.
2. Players call their own lines and the **server calls the score aloud before each first serve** –
   that is literally the scoreboard.
3. The interesting drama of unumpired tennis is therefore *disputes*: bad calls, ball-mark
   arguments on clay, calling the roving umpire over. Tennis Europe's Appendix G codifies exactly
   this: erase the mark and you concede the point; wrongly call "out" twice and you lose the point.
4. Coverage improves **as the draw narrows**, not as the tournament grows. The same tournament that
   published nothing on Monday can produce a live-scored, live-streamed final on Sunday.

### 2.2 Rights law – the feed is owned before it exists

Both the ITF (WTTJ Appendix M) and Tennis Europe (Appendix P) contain effectively identical
Data Rights appendices, and they are strict:

- "Official data" is defined to include order of play, draw, **live match scores and in-match
  incidents such as a point being scored**, and statistics.
- The federation holds the **exclusive live scoring rights** during the "match period" (start of
  match to 30 seconds after the last game).
- The host organiser may only use official data on its own site/app **after the match period** –
  in-venue scoreboards are the one live exception.
- Data rights protection is blunt: the host organiser must not allow "the dissemination,
  transmission, publication or release of any official data" (Tennis Europe Appendix P) from the
  venue, and the use of laptops and phones inside the venue to collect or transmit scores is
  **prohibited** except for credentialed personnel and incidental editorial use.

So an enthusiast typing live scores from the sideline of a J60 is not filling a gap the federation
tolerates – they are breaching the tournament's terms. This is exactly the same clause the pro tour
uses; the difference is that on the pro tour the federation then *exercises* the right and sells a
feed, while in juniors it mostly holds the right unexercised.

### 2.3 Safeguarding – and the widely-assumed thing that is NOT true

**Loud finding: there is no rule against publishing minors' names, ages, nationalities or scores in
tennis. The regulations mandate publishing them.**

- ITF WTTJ Regulation 63(c): players acknowledge "their tennis biography and complete tournament
  history shall be published" on the ITF's website, for transparency and promotion of the sport.
  Tennis Europe carries a word-for-word equivalent.
- Public draws are a *rule*: Tennis Europe requires draws to be made in public with at least two
  players or national coaches present, one from a different nation than the host.
- A 12-year-old's full name, nationality, date-linked results and tournament history are therefore
  public by design across Europe, the ITF and the national federations.

What safeguarding *does* restrict is **images and recordings**:

- ITF WTTJ Regulation 64 (*Player Images*) prohibits any covered person from obtaining,
  transmitting, storing or distributing **any images or recordings, audio or visual, of players**
  unless they are the parent/guardian, without express written authorisation from **both** the
  parent/guardian **and** the ITF Supervisor – and permitted material must be exclusively of
  matches, practice, or trophy presentations. Tennis Europe has the same clause for "Tournament
  Visitors".
- On the **professional** tour the same clause exists but is narrowed by age: the 2026 WTT
  Regulations restrict images of "junior players (being players who are under 18 years of age)".
  On the junior tour it applies to **all** players. That asymmetry is the clean demonstration that
  the rule is about minors, not about tennis.
- The ITF Safeguarding Children Policy (Jan 2025) requires "advance written parental consent for
  the taking and publication of photographs" or videos of children, including for performance
  analysis, bans photography in changing rooms and similar areas, and requires that only material
  shot by a covered person in an official capacity is used.

**Design consequence:** the constraint in junior tennis is on the *visual* channel, not the *textual*
one. A text feed naming a 14-year-old and describing her match is legal and normal. A phone video of
the same match by a stranger is a disciplinary offence. This is the single most counter-intuitive
finding in this document and it points the opposite way from the intuition that "you can't cover
kids in detail".

Note the one live-stream carve-out that reads as safeguarding-adjacent: any junior live stream must
display an on-screen graphic with the players' names – i.e. attribution is required, anonymity is
not.

### 2.4 Integrity and betting – coverage is deliberately suppressed at the bottom

The most decision-relevant precedent is on the pro side, and it runs the *opposite* way from
"more coverage is always better":

- The Tennis Integrity Review Panel recommended halting the sale of live scoring data at ITF $15k
  and $25k events because low-level live data is the substrate for match-fixing.
- In December 2019 the ITF confirmed it would **phase out live scoring data collection at events up
  to $15,000**, with the phase-out completing by end of 2021, alongside an $8m integrity
  investment. ITF President David Haggerty: "Our commitment to protecting the integrity of the
  World Tennis Tour is paramount."
- Junior regulations bake the same instinct in everywhere: live streams must not be made available
  for gambling purposes; official data may not be used in connection with gambling; tournaments may
  not carry betting-company sponsorship.

So the bottom of the ladder is *not* an untended gap that better technology would naturally fill.
Governing bodies have already once looked at cheap live data from unwatched matches involving
financially vulnerable players and concluded the correct amount of it was **less**. Juniors –
minors, unranked, unumpired, unwatched – are the extreme case of that same risk profile.

I could **not** verify a specific statutory ban on betting on under-18 tennis events (searched UK
Gambling Commission LCCP and ITIA material; the ITIA rules found concern *covered persons* betting,
not operators offering markets on juniors). Treat "betting on juniors is illegal" as unverified.

### 2.5 Economics – nobody is paid to be in the chair, let alone the press box

- The ITF's own Organisational Requirements make a Press Officer merely **recommended**, described
  as someone who issues press releases to local media and handles media requests. There is no
  requirement for any journalist, any media centre, any post-match interview infrastructure.
- Ball persons: recommended from semi-finals at J300/J500, required only for J500 finals. Below
  that, players fetch their own balls.
- Sanction fees run from $550 (J30/J60/J100) to $1,200 (Junior Grand Slam) – the entire commercial
  weight of a J30 is measured in hundreds of dollars.
- Media rights exist on paper (the ITF is exclusive owner of all audio/audio-visual rights) but in
  practice a national association wanting to stream must ask permission, geoblock domestically,
  impose a ≥30-second delay, hand the ITF a free copy, and assign copyright to the ITF at the end.
  That is a rights regime designed to *protect* a market that does not yet exist, and it is
  friction for the volunteer who might otherwise point a camera at a court.

The plain reading: coverage costs money, junior tennis has none, so the coverage that exists is the
coverage that costs nothing – draws, order of play, results, typed once.

### 2.6 Infrastructure – what physically is not there

- **No electronic line calling as standard.** ELC is *permitted* at ITF junior events but requires
  a request nine weeks in advance, ITF classification of the system, ITF approval, and the ITF can
  revoke it mid-tournament. Review (challenges) needs separate advance approval.
- **No ball tracking / no shot-level statistics.** Any player analysis technology (PAT) needs prior
  written ITF approval, and players and coaches may not operate their own ELC systems.
- **No line umpires** below the top categories; Tennis Europe does not require line umpires for
  European Junior Championships on clay at all.
- **Scoreboards are only "should provide"**, not required, in the ITF's junior organisational
  requirements.

Everything a modern pro text feed leans on – serve speed, ace counts, rally length, challenge
outcomes, break-point conversion – simply has no source at junior level. Even where a chair umpire
exists, the umpire records time, point/game/set score, server, and ends. That is the whole dataset.

### 2.7 Format – what a feed could even say

Junior formats are close to pro formats at the top and diverge at the bottom, and the divergence
changes the *shape* of a commentary line, not just its frequency.

ITF WTTJ 2026:
- Main draw singles (elimination): best of three tie-break sets – identical to the pro women's tour.
- Qualifying singles: 2 tie-break sets + 10-point match tie-break.
- **New for 2026:** J30/J60 round-robin-stage matches use 2 sets + 10-point match tie-break.
- Doubles: 2 tie-break sets + match tie-break, **No-Ad** scoring.

Tennis Europe 2026:
- 14/16&U and 12&U Cat 1 main draw: best of three tie-break sets.
- Cat 2/3 round-robin stage: 2 sets + 10-point match tie-break.
- Doubles everywhere: 2 tie-break sets, **No-Ad**, plus **No-Let** service rule at every event.
- Consolation ("Bonus") draws may be **best of three Short Sets** (to 4 games).
- Severe weather may convert a third set to a match tie-break mid-event.

Junior team competitions: short sets to 4 games + match tie-break are available as an abbreviated
format.

Implications for any text engine:
1. **No-Ad removes deuce entirely** – no advantage sequences, no marathon games, and it introduces
   a genuinely different dramatic beat: a single sudden-death point at 40-40 with the *receiver*
   choosing the return side.
2. **No-Let removes the let replay** – net-cord serves are live.
3. **Match tie-breaks compress the endgame** – the third-set narrative arc is replaced by a
   ten-point sprint.
4. **Short sets to 4** change what "a break" is worth by roughly double.
5. Formats can change *during* an event (weather, draw-size fallback), so the format is a per-match
   property, not a per-tournament one.
6. Because there is no line-calling technology, the natural junior-specific events for a text feed
   are **disputed calls, ball-mark checks, the roving umpire arriving, score disagreements** –
   things that literally cannot happen in a pro feed.

## 3. Where the line actually falls

It is **gradual, in three distinct steps, and each step is a different kind of thing**:

**Step 1 – result publication. Universal.** Every rung from a national U12 event upward publishes
draws, order of play and results into a tournament-software system. Even the smallest rung has
this. Latency is minutes to hours, granularity is set scores.

**Step 2 – live score. Appears mid-ladder, tied to the chair umpire.** In practice: Tennis Europe
Super Category and category events from the semi-finals; ITF J300 from the semi-finals, J500 from
the Round of 32; universal at the Junior Grand Slams because Slam courts are all umpired anyway.
Tennis Europe's IONSport rollout from summer 2026 is the first attempt to push this step *below*
the chair-umpire line by using verified crowd scoring.

**Step 3 – live video. Top of the junior ladder only.** J500 quarter-finals onwards (mandatory, 2
courts), J300 recommended, LTA/USTA national finals, junior Slams via the host broadcaster's
all-courts streams, and college tennis via team-submitted streams and Cracked Racquets.

**Step 4 – live text commentary. Does not exist.** I found no junior event at any level, including
Junior Grand Slam finals, with a ball-by-ball written feed. What does exist is **retrospective
text**: ITF previews and recaps, LTA previews, federation news pieces, and one specialist
independent blog – ZooTennis (Colette Lewis), publishing daily on junior and college tennis since
2005, which is essentially the world's junior-tennis wire service and is still end-of-day recap
form, not live. Her verdict on the 2026 J500 rule change, with appropriate scepticism about whether
it gets implemented: "I applaud the ITF for requiring the J500s to provide live scoring".

**So: does a junior Grand Slam final get text commentary when a J100 first round does not?** No.
The junior Slam final gets a live score, a live stream and a report written afterwards. The gap
between them is a gap in *data and video*, not in *narrative*. Nobody writes live prose about
juniors at all.

## 4. What a player, parent or coach actually sees today

Reconstructed from federation regulations, tournament-software behaviour, vendor products and tennis
parent sources. This is the practical texture the game should reproduce.

**Before the day.** A tennis parent's most reliable source is a draw sheet on a website, and there
is more than one candidate website. A ParentingAces guide to ITF junior events describes draws and
times as possibly being on the ITF junior site, the tournament's own site, or the national
association's site, requiring some digging. Order of play is published the evening before –
Tennis Europe targets 19:00 local, and it is also **displayed physically at the venue**.

**Sign-in.** Physical, on site, with passport/photo ID and IPIN. Nothing about junior tournament
participation is remote.

**During the match.** Almost always **nothing**. The parent is either courtside watching, or
watching another court, or refreshing a draw page that will not change until somebody types the
result in. There is no chair umpire to produce data on most courts, and the venue's own rules
prohibit collecting and transmitting scores from the site. The physical order-of-play board and the
tournament desk are the real interfaces.

**Where the live layer does exist**, it is one of:
- a federation stream from one or two show courts (J500 QF+, LTA national finals, junior Slams);
- an official live score at the top of a draw (J500 from R32, junior Slams);
- a **third-party app scored by a parent or fan** – IONSport's Verified Crowd Scoring, Match Tennis
  App's user-submitted scores, ScoreBuzzer's player self-scoring mode. This is the single most
  under-appreciated fact about how junior tennis is followed: much of the "live scoring" that
  families see is being typed in **by another family**.

**After.** Result posts to the draw, ranking points process (Tennis Europe rankings update weekly on
Tuesdays), and the player's full tournament history becomes permanently public on the federation
site.

**College is the exception a junior notices immediately.** A US college dual match may have a
stream, a live team scoreboard, a broadcast crew, and a crowd. A junior arriving from a J100
experiences that as a step change – arguably the biggest jump in visibility anywhere in a player's
pre-professional life, and it happens *sideways* out of the junior pyramid, not upward through it.

## 5. Findings that contradict common assumptions

1. **"Minors' names can't be published."** False, and strongly so. Publication of full biography and
   complete tournament history is a condition of entry at both the ITF and Tennis Europe. Draws must
   be made publicly. The restriction is on **images and recordings**, which require written parental
   consent *and* Supervisor authorisation.
2. **"The ITF live-scores its junior tour."** Not meaningfully. Live scoring is *encouraged*
   everywhere and *required* only at J500 where a chair umpire is present. The ITF's own J500
   tournament page has no live scores tab, and its live-scoring app is marketed for the professional
   entry and mid-levels.
3. **"Junior Slams get proper coverage."** They get proper *scores and video* because they are inside
   a pro event. They do not get live text. The editorial treatment is a preview and a recap.
4. **"Coverage gaps are just neglect and will be filled by technology."** Partly the opposite: the
   ITF deliberately **removed** live scoring data from its lowest professional rung in 2019–2021 for
   integrity reasons, and both junior rulebooks contain clauses prohibiting the transmission of
   scores from the venue. Some of the silence is policy.
5. **"There's no live scoring because there's no umpire, therefore it's impossible."** No longer
   true as of 2026. Tennis Europe + IONSport are deploying **verified crowd scoring** across ~540
   events precisely to produce a feed without a chair umpire. Junior live data is arriving via the
   crowd, not via officials.
6. **"College tennis is covered because it's better tennis."** It's covered because universities have
   media departments, streams and fans. The ITA's live scores/streams listing is *submitted by
   member teams* – it is opt-in institutional effort, not a governing-body feed.

## 6. Explicitly not verified

- Whether the ITF's public live-score product (live.itftennis.com, which redirects to
  itftennis.com/en/world-tennis-tour-live) actually carries junior matches. The site navigation
  lists "World Tennis Tour Juniors" among tours, but the page is JavaScript-rendered and I could
  not load the live board itself; the J500 tournament page has no live scores tab, and the app
  listing does not mention juniors. **Conflicting evidence, unresolved.**
- Whether aggregators (Sofascore, Flashscore/Livesport, LiveScore) carry point-by-point for junior
  Grand Slam matches, or only set scores. They clearly carry junior Slam draws and results.
- Whether Wimbledon's all-court electronic line calling (from 2025) covers junior matches
  specifically. Reporting says "all match courts" including qualifying; juniors are not named.
- ScoreBuzzer's claim to power live scoring for "ITF Juniors". This is a vendor claim on its own

- Whether the USTA offers any official live scoring for junior tournaments. I found draws, results,

- Any statutory prohibition on betting markets for under-18 tennis events.
- The exact capture granularity of IONSport's Verified Crowd Scoring (point-by-point is claimed in
  its store listing; the Tennis Europe press release does not specify the scoring method).

## 7. What this implies for Ties Break

Short version, for the commentary/text system:

1. **Text commentary at junior level is a game invention, not a simulation of reality.** That is
   fine, but it should be *framed* as the player's own perception rather than as a broadcast feed –
   because there is no broadcast feed and the player would know it.
2. **The realistic progression to model is data availability, not prose quality.** Rung 1: a draw
   sheet that updates hours later. Rung 2: a score that moves when a chair umpire exists (semi-final
   onwards, then earlier and earlier). Rung 3: a stream from one show court. Rung 4 (pro): the full
   feed. A player climbing from 14 to the tour should *feel themselves become visible*, in that
   order.
3. **The single most authentic junior-tennis mechanic is self-officiating.** Bad calls, ball marks,
   the server calling the score, "can you show me the mark", the roving umpire arriving four courts
   late. No pro-tennis text engine can generate that. It is free character.
4. **No-Ad, No-Let, match tie-breaks and short sets are per-match properties at youth level** and
   they change the beat structure of a match. If the engine assumes deuce/advantage everywhere it
   will feel wrong for junior doubles and consolation draws in particular.
5. **Nobody is watching, and that is the point.** The emotional content of a J30 first round is that
   the result exists only as a line on a draw sheet that three people will look at. Coverage arriving
   is itself an achievement worth dramatising.
6. **Photography/video is restricted but text is not** – if the game ever models media, press or
   social attention, the realistic early-career restriction is on *footage*, with parental and
   supervisor consent as the gate.
