# What tennis players actually do about aircraft (owner's research, 03.09.2026)

⚠ Supplied by the owner as reference, not as a ruling. Filed because the shop's `plane` family was
priced before anyone had looked at the real market, and this is the first time we have.

## Outright ownership is RARE, and the one clear case prices our own small plane

* **Rafael Nadal – Cessna Citation CJ2+.** About **$5–7M**, bought outright in 2010. A light jet
  carrying **up to 8 passengers**, flown between Mallorca and the European tournaments.
* **Ion Tiriac – a whole fleet (Tiriac Air).** $10M to $50M+ per airframe; Bombardier Global 5000
  and Gulfstream G200. He founded a charter company – a billionaire ex-player, not a touring one.

⭐ **Our small plane at $7M lands on Nadal's own aircraft**, which is the closest real analogue a
touring professional has. The number was chosen before this research and survives it.

## What the rest of the top actually use: fractional ownership

* **Roger Federer – Bombardier Challenger 350 via NetJets.** The jet is $25–27M new, but he has
  never owned one: he has been a NetJets partner and its face since 2004 and holds a FRACTION,
  which buys him a jet anywhere on demand.
* **Novak Djokovic – Bombardier Learjet 45 and others.** No confirmed outright aircraft. Years as a
  Bombardier ambassador, roughly 200 hours a year on their jets, plus NetJets for the team.

⭐⭐⭐ **The finding worth keeping, and it is a mechanic rather than a price:** the shop offers only
one shape – buy the whole thing and carry its upkeep for ever. The real tour's answer is
**fractional ownership or a long-term charter contract**, precisely because it takes the maintenance
off the player. Our `upkeepBps` is the cost that makes the plane a burden; a fractional rung would
be the choice that answers it, and it would be true to the sport rather than invented.

## The new generation refuses it outright, which is a story the game does not tell

* **Carlos Alcaraz** flies scheduled commercial on principle and has publicly said he turned down a
  private-jet sponsorship.
* **Jannik Sinner and Daniil Medvedev** take one-off charters (a Hawker 400XP, say) shared with
  other players or with the tournament, when a city has to be reached quickly.

⭐ So the honest ladder is not «no plane → plane». It is **scheduled → shared charter → a fraction →
outright**, and only the last rung is what the shop currently sells. ⚠ Not proposed for this wave;
recorded because the shop's `air` category is the thinnest in the game and this is what it is
missing.
