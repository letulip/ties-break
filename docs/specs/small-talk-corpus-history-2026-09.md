# The small-talk corpus – the review history

⚠⚠ **THIS FILE EXISTS SO THE LIVE CORPUS STOPS CARRYING ITS OWN REJECTED DRAFT.** His 17.09 review:
«the file is now a development journal, historical audit, corpus, benchmark report and current
specification in one document … it preserves the rejected S1–S12 material alongside the corrected
corpus, meaning future searches and AI retrieval will repeatedly surface wording the document itself
rejected.»

He is right, and the retrieval risk is the sharp half: a rejected line in a spec is a line an agent
will find and reuse. The live corpus is
[small-talk-corpus-2026-09](small-talk-corpus-2026-09.md); what follows is the record of how it got
there – the twelve first-pass situations, why each failed, and both of his reviews.

## 7. What this document does not do

* **It writes no replies.** Each situation shows the parent's three labels; what she says back to each
  is written after he has cut and corrected the openers, because **144** replies (12 × 4 × 3) to situations
  that may not survive a reading is work spent on the wrong thing.
* **It ships nothing.** Every line here is a DRAFT under invariant 4.
* **It does not fix the repeat.** That is round 43 #8(a) – the exclusion read off `world.lifeLog` –
  and it is architect's work with no schema and no copy. A bigger corpus makes a repeat rarer; only
  the exclusion makes a BACK-TO-BACK repeat impossible.
* **It proposes an engine change it has not measured**, because there is nothing to measure: the
  four-voice restructure is a data-shape change, and its whole effect is the grid table in §6.
