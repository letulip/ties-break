# The localization rig – technical rails under the owner's editorial stack

Status: DRAFT spec (L0), 01.10.2026. Owner rulings pending – §9.
Commissioned 01.10: «давай посмотрим как нам лучше подойти к локализации, пока я делаю перевод…
поймем что нужно сделать, чтобы это работало корректно и без сбоев, т.к. у нас очень большой слой
текста, это основная часть игры».

## 1 · The two halves, and which one this spec is

Localization is two projects that must not be confused:

- **The editorial half is the owner's and already running.** Branch `codex/localization-ru`
  carries `docs/localization/`: a README with the working contract, a style guide, a source
  inventory, an LQA worksheet and per-batch replacement tables with the status vocabulary
  `DRAFT → QUESTION → APPROVED → LANDED`. Dated owner rulings live there (01.10: `Дом`, `Рейтинг`,
  `ё` everywhere, the full-coverage rule, the product mark stays English, `вы` from the interface /
  `ты` from the daughter). That stack is the source of truth for every Russian line. Nothing in
  this spec invents wording – invariant 4 stands.
  ⚙ INTAKE 07.10 ([review 15](../review-codex/15-localization-ru-response.md)): the stack is
  RU-01…RU-17 now – some forty batch tables, 61 files, drafted against `d69ff15d` (30.09) with its
  own delta process (RU-15/16/17) against `1e7b125b` (06.10). Rounds 47/48 landed after its audit
  base; the measured string drift is the review's §3 and becomes **RU-18**, his table to fill
  before the ending/album landing batches run.
- **The technical half is this spec**: the catalog, the lookup, the formatters, the import from his
  tables, the gates that keep a 55k-word corpus from rotting, and the engine-side change that makes
  stored narrative translatable at all.

The denominator (copy census, 30.09, `tools/copy-census.ts`): CERTAIN 3,709 strings / 34,724 words
(3,097 unique), LIKELY 1,684 / 16,838 words, the childhood prologue ≈1,968 words – ≈55k words,
≈5k unique strings, interpolation in 10.1% of strings, concentrated in ledger sentences and Vue
template text. Languages: RU first (the owner authors), ES later (his ruling: only through a
trusted human translator), both on the same rails.

## 2 · Where the text lives – three classes, three treatments

The census and a source read give three mechanically different classes:

**(a) Render-time UI copy** – `.vue` template text and attrs (`title`, `aria-label`,
`placeholder`, `alt`), literal props, screen labels. Rendered fresh every frame; translating it is
a lookup at the call site. The biggest class by count.

**(b) Engine copy rendered at snapshot time** – life-beat prompts (`lifeBeatPromptFor(world, row)`
assembles on read), match commentary, offer/knock texts, ECONOMY labels. Not persisted as prose;
re-renders on every snapshot, so it switches language the moment the renderer knows the locale.

**(c) State-baked prose** – `WorldEvent.text` ledger sentences, letters in the inbox, diary/album
entries: composed once, stored in the save, displayed verbatim forever. This is the hard class.
The owner's ruling 4 (01.10, in his README) decides its fate: «legacy English stored in
`WorldEvent.text` may be retained internally for save compatibility, but it is not an acceptable
visible fallback in Russian mode». So stored prose cannot stay the display source – see §5.

## 3 · Architecture

### 3.1 Keys: the English literal, plus a context tag where one English string needs two translations

The runtime key for a string is its English source literal (with `{…}` placeholders), optionally
prefixed by a short context tag where the same English must translate differently
(`nav|Stats` vs `heading|Stats` – his RU-01-N04 note already reserves exactly this split). This is
the gettext `msgctxt` shape, chosen for four measured reasons:

- **Fallback is readable English, never a key id.** Partial coverage degrades to the shipped game,
  which makes every intermediate state shippable and every miss visible in play.
- **The whole-literal strings-roundtrip pins survive.** The literal stays in source as the key, so
  the seven comment-stripped roundtrip tables keep matching without a re-pin wave.
- **The census walker is the extractor.** `tools/copy-census.ts` already parses every string with
  the real parsers (TS AST, `vue/compiler-sfc`, live ECONOMY walk); the catalog generator reuses
  that walk instead of a second, disagreeing scanner.
- **His tables join on English.** The editorial rows carry `source + English + Russian`; English
  text is the join column that survives line-number drift between his baseline and moving main.

His contract says «English text is evidence, never a localization key» – that is an editorial
statement (Russian is written from the fact, not word-for-word) and stays true; technically the
English literal is merely the lookup handle, and his editorial ids (RU01-N03…) are never required
to match it.

### 3.2 The three call shapes

- UI: `t('Start a new career')`, `t('Week {week} of {total}', { week, total })` – a thin composable
  over the shared core; templates keep their literal visible at the call site.
- Engine: `` cp`Rain washed out ${name}'s practice` `` – a tagged template producing a
  **CopyRef** `{ k: "Rain washed out {0}'s practice", p: [name] }`. The engine never renders prose
  for the player again; it emits CopyRefs, and the UI renders them under the current locale. The
  migration diff per call site is one backtick prefix, which is what makes class (b)/(c) migration
  mechanical and parity-checkable.
- Params may themselves be CopyRefs (letters hold paragraphs); the renderer recurses.

### 3.3 The core: ~150 lines, zero new runtime dependencies

`src/shared/i18n.ts` (engine-importable, no Vue): the CopyRef type and a message formatter
supporting plain interpolation plus an ICU subset – `{n, plural, one{…} few{…} many{…} other{…}}`
and `{g, select, f{…} m{…}}` – with plural categories from `Intl.PluralRules` and numbers through
`Intl.NumberFormat`. English sources never need ICU (English plural forks already exist as
separate literals); Russian values use it where RU grammar demands (few/many, gender agreement).
`src/i18n/` (UI layer): the reactive locale, `t()`, catalog loading – RU/ES dictionaries are
lazy-imported chunks, English costs nothing because the key renders itself.

Gender: where a Russian value needs `select` on a person the English sentence never mentions, the
call site must pass the gender param. The extractor cannot know which rows need it; the editorial
table marks them (his contract 4, «which grammatical form the sentence needs»), and the landing
wave adds the param per marked row.

⚠ MARKUP-IN-MESSAGES IS A NAMED OPEN DESIGN (L2-6, 08.10). Five wired-around cuts cannot reach
Russian word order while a message cannot carry a bold/semantic segment: the debt countdown, the
household in/out/short figures, the rehab note's `<b>`, the rung price, the contract term line –
plus RU-12A D17's «for life» bold slot. The ICU subset stays text-only in L2; the design (rich
segments in values, or component-split messages) is an L3/L4 decision with the owner's eye on it.

⚠ CASE FORMS ARE AN EXPLICIT NON-GOAL OF L1 (intake 07.10). The style guide's §5 wants
prepositional/instrumental/genitive off data eventually, and itself rules the bridge: «prefer a
sentence that does not demand inflection until the technical layer carries the needed form». The
ICU subset stays plural + gender select; case support is revisited after the first phone LQA, with
the guide's own uninflected-shape law carrying until then.

### 3.4 Locale is app state, never career state

The chosen language lives with the other app-level preferences, is switchable at any time, and is
never written into a save. ⚙ RULED 07.10: TWO entry points – the More setting by the save
controls, and a **first-run locale prompt** («на первом экране надо предлагать сразу выбор языка,
может даже до всего остального для первого входа»): shown once before the splash flow on a fresh
install, never again after a choice; both write the same preference. The engine's dice do not know it exists: a twin-run gate (same seed,
EN vs RU) must show identical `rngMain {s,n}` and an identical numeric snapshot. Input-independence
law, applied to language.

## 4 · The pipeline: his tables in, a catalog out, both generated and gated

- `tools/i18n-extract.ts` (census walker reused) emits `src/i18n/catalog.en.json`: every key with
  its home file, placeholder list (with the source expression as a semantic hint), and census area.
  Generated and committed; `npm run check` regenerates in a clean tree and fails on drift – the
  registry pattern.
- `tools/i18n-import.ts` reads `docs/localization/*.md` replacement tables and compiles rows with
  status `APPROVED`/`LANDED` into `src/i18n/ru.json`. Join by English text with the location as a
  tiebreak hint; every row that no longer matches a live key is reported (his baseline is
  `d69ff15d`, main moves – drift is expected and must be visible, never silent). `DRAFT` rows
  compile nowhere and count as pending.
- `npm run i18n:check`, in `check`: catalog fresh; ru.json ⊆ catalog (orphans listed);
  placeholder parity between key and value (a dropped `{name}` in a translation is the classic
  breakage); ICU syntax parses; plural values carry every category the locale requires; coverage
  printed per editorial batch. ⚙ Two additions from the intake (07.10): a `—`-in-Russian-values
  lint (the branch's own RU-14 ask – 141 cells were hand-fixed once; the machine holds the line),
  and a **same-English-many-surfaces flag**: `Spent` died on the ending screen while staying live
  on two other surfaces, so a row whose English key matches several catalog homes is reported as
  needing a context tag rather than silently joined (review 15 §3.1, the trap). A row whose Russian
  EQUALS its English source by ruling (§9.5's Latin name rows) compiles as identity – lawful, never
  reported as untranslated; the two RECORD files (`ru-main-delta-2`, `ru-family-voice-pass`) are
  skipped entirely, their old columns quote retired wording on purpose.
- Every later wave's PR lists its new keys (the catalog diff) – the moving-target treaty with the
  translator: he always sees what appeared since his last pass, the way DRAFT string tables already
  work.

`LANDED` stays an editorial status in his docs; the importer's report says which APPROVED rows are
live in code. ⚙ RULED 07.10 (§9.1): at thousands of rows the flip is mechanised – the importer's
`--mark-landed` write-back rewrites APPROVED→LANDED in the tables for rows verified live, one
command per batch; the command is his to run (or lands in a PR he merges), so the authorization
never leaves him while the hand-edits do.

⚙ **As shipped, L1b (08.10)** – what the L2-n builders need to know:

- **A key exists when the code asks for it.** The catalog is the census's CERTAIN strings (still
  unwrapped) plus every `t('…')` and `` cp`…` `` call site (wrapped, flagged `wrapped`). Holes are
  positional – `{0}`, `{1}` – exactly what `cp` builds, and the source expression behind each hole is kept
  as a hint (`ph`). Home is the FILE, never a line, so an unrelated edit does not move the catalog.
  `t(variable)` is invisible to any extractor; the gate prints how many such calls there are. A call
  site passes positional params as an array – `t('Week {0} of {1}', [week, total])` – so the key it
  writes is the key the catalog already holds; a call site that prefers names writes its own spelling,
  the next `npm run i18n:extract` takes it, and the importer re-adapts his placeholders by position.
- **A short label in a script is not a key until it is wrapped.** The census leaves them out by its own
  recall rule (`{ label: 'Home' }`), so the APPROVED rows `Home`, `Wealthy`, `Middle class`,
  `Working class` and `Dynasty` are `waiting` until a landing writes `t('Home')` – in a computed, so the
  label follows the locale. `Stats` is `foreign-surface`: the catalog holds the screen heading, his
  ruling is for the nav label, which needs its own call site (`t('nav|Stats')` – the tag lives at the
  call site, §9.2). `W14 '31` is a formatter example, not a string.
- **Dispositions, not silence.** Every APPROVED/LANDED row ends as compiled, waiting, foreign-surface,
  unmatched (drift), ambiguous, conflict or exempt-latin; DRAFT rows count as pending. The report prints
  them all, and the gate fails only on a stale generated file, a ru.json defect, a conflict or the
  record-file tripwire – drift among DRAFT rows is a number, not a failure.
- **`--mark-landed` flips a row only when its key is wired (`wrapped`) AND ru.json carries the value.**
  LANDED means wired in code; a catalog entry alone is not that.
- **The importer never reads the two record files**, and the gate fails if a name on its skip list stops
  existing on disk.
- **`ru` is a real catalog from here on** (`src/i18n/ru.json`, a lazy chunk). The L1a cases that used it as
  «the locale with no file» now use a made-up code (`zz`), and the `AppRoot` flow cases register an empty
  stand-in loader – a test that needs a language with no file must not borrow one that is about to get
  its file (Spanish, L5). Every wave that adds or rewords a player-facing string now commits the
  `npm run i18n:extract` diff with it, or `check` fails on the stale catalog.

⚙ **As shipped, L3-T (10.10)** – the tooling the L2/L3 waves parked, closed with no engine file, no save and no word of copy touched:

- **A dynamic seat is declared, not guessed** (`tools/i18n-seats.ts`). A seat is a key the code ASKS for without writing it at the call site: a **call seat** (a screen reads a label off the snapshot, `t(option.label)`) or a **ref seat** (the engine writes `c: { k: line }` beside a corpus string; the string is its own key). The registry names each seat's key set as CODE – a `groups()` function that walks the real constants (`LIFE_BEAT_OPTIONS`, `SMALL_TALK_SITUATIONS`, `FRIDGE_NOTES`, `ALBUM_CORPUS`, the gift `BANDS` through `BIRTHDAY_BANDS`), so no English string is retyped – grouped by the file that holds it. 13 seats (5 call seats: the life-beat dialog's `option.label`, `replying.done` and `prompt.confirm`, the moment overlay's `moment.confirm`, the calendar's `fridgeNote`; 8 ref seats: the gifts, the diary's pool, debut lines, week notes and travel notes, the small-talk corpus and its frames, the album corpus) reach 2,240 catalog keys. The extractor marks a reached key `wrapped`, records the seat ids beside it (`seat: […]` – additive, the catalog format stays 1) and gives it the constant's file as a home; a string no census rule reads ENTERS the catalog (the 159 gift strings: 5,226 → 5,385 keys, `OUTSIDE_CATALOG.gifts` 159 → 0). So `--mark-landed` flips an APPROVED row whose key is seat-reachable: wired-via-seat = wired. The census is still ONE walker – it now also keeps the *sites* of its unreadable `t()` calls (`dynamicSites`) so a declaration can be held against them.
- **A declaration the tree does not back marks nothing.** A call seat names its site (file + the first argument's source) and the extractor demands the walker found it; a ref seat names its writer (file + the text of the ref) and demands the file still says it. A stale seat is a gate RED (`seat-stale`) and enters no key; a key that cannot be a seat key (`{`, `}`, `\` or a context-tag prefix) is `seat-unseatable`; an empty key set is `seat-empty`. A dynamic `t()` call that NO seat declares is printed by the gate (`5 dynamic t() calls: 5 declared, 0 unreadable`) and pinned at zero by `tests/i18n-l3-t-tooling.test.ts` §2 – a new `t(variable)` arrives with its seat or fails that net. Named, not forgotten: the hub's own pools, L3-1's named-constant sinks and L3-7's commentary pools are catalog keys already and wired by nothing the extractor can see until their batch is approved – one registry entry each.
- **The walker folds `+`.** `t('a long lede, ' + 'kept whole')` is ONE key in a script and in a template (the template reader follows the chain; before, it recorded the first half as a key – a fragment nobody asked for). A `+` with anything but plain literals in it stays a dynamic call, counted and listed. No `t()` in `src` is such a chain today (the catalog the change left is byte-identical); the fold is for the next long lede.
- **A formatter's output is a PATTERN row, recognised by the formatter's own output** (`tools/i18n-formats.ts`). The APPROVED short-year row (`W14 '31`, §9.9d) has no key to join – its English is what `weekLabel` prints for one week. ⭐ **THE DESIGN FORK, and the lighter side taken:** the brief named a `format:` marker in the English cell or a hint cell naming the formatter; both mean editing a table he reads and approves in. Instead `FORMAT_EXAMPLES` calls the REAL formatter on a documented sample (`weekLabel(13, 2031)`, `weekYearLabel(26, 2033)`) and declares the sample's named parts (`week`, `yy`; `week`, `year`); a row whose English equals a registered example IS that formatter's row. **His tables are not touched and the importer's contract with them does not change**, and the drift alarm survives: if `weekLabel`'s shape moves, the registered example moves with it and the row is `unmatched` again. His Russian example becomes a pattern by finding each part's number exactly once, as a whole number (a repeated or missing one is `ambiguous-cell`, with the reason) and writing `{name}` in its place; the result is an ordinary message over named arguments and compiles into **`src/i18n/formats.ru.json`**, the formatter-locale table – generated, committed and gated like ru.json (`formats-stale`; an unregistered id, a dropped or invented part, a long dash, a pattern that does not parse). Why its own file and not a reserved namespace of ru.json: «ru.json ⊆ catalog» is a law of the gate and a pattern is not a key (no code asks for the English `W{week} '{yy}`; the formatter builds it); a separate file keeps both statements true, costs the bundle nothing until a consumer imports it (the lazy glob matches `ru.json` / `es.json` by name), and is where the L4 display shells will look. A key still wins (a row whose English is also a catalog key joins as before); a DRAFT row that names an example is `pending`, not `pending-dead`; two approved rows that disagree on one formatter conflict like two on one key. **Effect: the importer's `unmatched` 1 → 0 and `format` 1; ru.json did not move.** ⚠ The CONSUMERS are not built: `weekLabel` still prints English under every locale, nothing imports `formats.ru.json`, and `--mark-landed` leaves the row APPROVED and says why – the round that wires RU-13D's display shells reads the table.
- **The score tail is a rule of the gate.** The four kid-match keys (`{0}: {1} beat | lost to | retired against | beat a retiring {2} {3}`) end in the score hole and the bracket plaque splits a row at its score. `SCORE_TAIL_KEYS` in `tools/i18n-check.ts` names them; `lintRu` fails (`score-tail`) a locale value that does not END with `{3}`, exactly once – a word after it, the score in the middle, a trailing space, a doubled or escaped score – and `lintScoreTailTable` holds the table against the catalog (`score-tail-table`) while the net holds it against the source (a fifth kid-match key must arrive in the table).
- **Two of the seven debts live where their readers live.** The frozen-record capture (`tests/component/identity-capture.ts`): the inbox and shop records read a region as its text NODES, each trimmed, joined by one space, so a paragraph that becomes an interpolation stops moving goldens; both records were re-recorded once under it (every `text` leaf moved – 16 and 38 – each one whitespace at a node boundary and nothing else, proven per leaf; counts, order, controls, style probes and tree hashes byte-identical). And `tTransparent`'s context tag: `t('nav|Stats')` reads `'Stats'` for a source-shape pin (only the `splitContext` tag shape folds); `round13-nav`'s hand-rolled tag strip is gone and its pin goes red without the fold.

## 5 · Class (c): stored prose becomes CopyRef, and legacy saves follow

New events store `{ c: CopyRef }` instead of rendered text; display renders under the current
locale. That is a save-schema change: `SAVE_SCHEMA_VERSION` bump, append-only migration, golden
fixture – the three-part move, once.

Legacy saves hold English prose with their params already interpolated. The set of templates old
code could have produced is closed – old saves cannot contain strings future code writes – so the
migration carries a **frozen legacy-template table** (the pre-i18n literals, snapshotted into
`src/engine/migrations/` and never edited) and reverse-matches each stored `text` back to
`{ k, p }`. By construction the match rate should be ~100%; the unmatched remainder keeps its
`text` and is counted, and the count is the measured distance from ruling 4, reported to the owner
rather than hidden. Matching is pure string work against a frozen table – deterministic, no draws,
migration-lawful.

The parity gate for every migrated class: a 150-week twin career, old arm vs new arm, English –
every player-visible rendered string byte-identical, `rngMain` identical. The refactor may change
representation, never copy.

⚙ RULED 07.10 (§9.3): the migration goes ahead – and the plan-B is on record, his words: old
saves are not today's priority, and if the measured unmatched remainder disappoints, a
«продолжить или начать заново» dialog on loading a pre-i18n save into RU mode replaces the
reverse-match promise. The remainder NUMBER decides which ships; nobody hides it either way.

⚠⚠ ONE STORED TEXT IS BEHAVIOUR, NOT ONLY DISPLAY (intake 07.10, the branch's own P1 finding,
CONFIRMED): `src/engine/spirit.ts:1415` gates first-of-season exposure on `e.text === EXPOSURE_ROW`
– translate the stored row and the dedup breaks. The row already carries `lifeKind: 'exposure'`,
so the fix is a one-comparison swap to the semantic field, byte-identical worlds, before ANY
class-(c) work touches stored prose. That is pre-task **L1c** in §8. The sweep found no second
behaviour-bearing text identity in the engine; the L3 pin-query step re-checks per class anyway.

⚙ **THE TWO LEVERS L3 LEFT UNPULLED – decisions for L4 and the owner, each with the number it was measured at (10.10, L3-T).** Neither is needed to ship RU, both are prices L3 paid and ways to take part of one back, and neither is pulled here because both are one-way doors for saves.

1. **The text-drop lever.** Every converted class-(c) row stores its sentence twice: the English `text` and the ref `c` that renders to it (the pair `tests/i18n-l3-1-ledger-writers.test.ts` holds isomorphic, 170 pairs). L3-7 measured the whole layer's price on five careers: the world JSON is **+8.00% over the same worlds without refs at 150 weeks (gzip +4.75%) and +7.28% at 300 weeks (gzip +4.59%)** – the rows being match rows for the most part (L3-3's finding), the last wave's own increment +0.086% / +0.048%. The lever is to stop storing `text` on a row whose `c` renders to it and derive it at read. The saving is bounded above by that price; the real figure is whatever the dropped bytes measure (a seat repeats its string, so most of the +8.00% raw is `text`-sized, and gzip already folds the duplicate – which is why the gzip price is the smaller number and the gzip saving will be too). **Identity-era consequences** – while English is the only locale `text === render(c, en)`, so the lever is invisible and the twin can prove it; what changes is what stops being true: (a) `text` becomes optional for good – the v93 reverse-match leaves 1.73% of the owner's real save as dated wording with no `c`, so EVERY reader of a stored row (the L3-0 list: the plaque's `endsWith(score)` split, `tierFromLabel`, the greeting's collision rule, and every pin that reads `e.text`) must handle both shapes forever, not for one release; (b) the direction is one-way – a save written without `text` shows nothing in a build that predates the lever, so it is a schema move (invariant 3's three parts), not a refactor; (c) the L3-1 pair net (`text` ≡ render(`c`)) and §5's parity gate change what they compare – the stored string goes, the rendered string stays; (d) every golden fixture that carries a row re-aims, and the pin query for `e.text` has not been run – count it before pricing it. **Decision:** pull it only if a save-size problem is ever MEASURED; until then +8.00% raw / +4.59% gzip is the price of keeping English byte-identical and every old reader working.
2. **The heirloom ref-strip.** A generation-two career carries the mother's finished album INSIDE its save (`world.legacy.heirloomAlbum`, typed `unknown`, copied at the door), so L3-6's refs are stored beside its strings: a posed 8-sheet book measured **6,696 → 10,923 bytes (+63%, +4,227 bytes) on that field, on a generation-two save only**, while the eight ended careers' world JSON as a whole moved +0.17%. The lever is to strip the refs from the stored book at the door. **Identity-era consequences:** stripping is lawful today – a book stored by older code already carries strings only and draws its English under every locale, the identity mapping, proven in L3-6 – but it freezes the daughter's inherited album in English under RU for good (one screen on a rare path: the dynasty's second generation). Keeping the refs has a cost of its own, reasoned from the core's miss rule (§3.3, §6) rather than measured: a stored ref is a class-(b) key, and no append-only table protects those (the frozen legacy table covers class (c)) – reword a corpus line after a generation-two save exists and that save's stored ref misses the catalog and renders the OLD English under RU. **Decision for the owner:** whether a translated heirloom is worth 4.2 KB per generation-two save and that exposure. A finding rides with it: `AlbumNoteCard.vue` draws the checklist only when a note has no prose, so the heirloom's numbers are on the wire and in the save but on no screen today.

## 6 · The failure modes the gates exist for

- **Overflow.** Russian runs long; the phone-height law (375x667, the TourBriefingDialog lesson)
  already binds dialogs. A generated pseudo-locale `xx` (every catalog entry bracketed and padded
  ~30%) runs the mounted dialog suite before any real translation exists, so layout breaks are
  found by machine, not by his phone. His contract 7 (rendered phone LQA per batch) sits on top.
- **Hardcode leaks.** Under `xx`, mounted key screens are swept for text nodes without the bracket
  marker – an unmigrated literal shows itself. Allowlist for numbers, names, the product mark.
- **Runtime misses.** In RU mode a missing key renders English and increments a counter; the LQA
  pass (an e2e sweep under `locale=ru`) asserts the counter is zero for the batches he has marked
  done. Ruling 4, as a number.
- **Determinism.** The EN-vs-RU twin gate (§3.4) and the parity gate (§5), both draw-free.
