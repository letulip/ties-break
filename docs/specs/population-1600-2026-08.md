# A tour of 1,600 – the population the sport has, and the ladder that tapers

**Status: §0 (the ship rule) written before any code was touched. Everything below it is measured.**

The owner's instruction, verbatim: *«довести население до ~1 600, как в реальной WTA – да, со
старением, сменами, всё как у нас было.»* The real WTA list holds about 1,550 women
(`docs/research/real-ladder-pace.md` §3b); we model 520.

`docs/specs/ladder-pace-2026-08.md` §6D wrote this wave's own arithmetic down before it was ordered:
*"For W15's shipped band `[0.22, 0.72]` to reach the real #400+, the merged table needs ~1,800 rows –
`FIELD.size` ≈ 1,600, which is the real WTA's own ~1,550."* And §6C listed the three things that have
to move with a strength compression. Both are executed here, as separately measured steps.

The second half of the wave is the owner's approved calibration target, quoted verbatim:

| rung | title chance |
| --- | --- |
| W15 / W35 | **15–35%** |
| W50 / W75 / W100 | **10–20%** |
| WTA 125 / 250 | **5–12%** |
| 500 / 1000 | **2–6%** |
| Slam | rare – an event of a career |

with his reasoning, which governs the design: *«иначе это вообще боль, у нас всё-таки игра»* – and
against it the counter-pressure, which is that a flat band everywhere makes the rung she stands on
meaningless. **The table is deliberately two to five times more generous than the sport**
(`real-ladder-pace.md` §2c measures a real future top-100 at 15.2 ITF events per title = **6.6%**).
That is recorded here as a decision, not an oversight.

### The six criteria

**The wave ships only if ALL SIX hold. Any one fails and the step that broke it is reported and
reverted, exactly as `FIELD.earnCurve` and `FIELD.strengthCurve` were.** Each step is measured on its
own arm so its effect is attributable.

1. **CORRESPONDENCE DOES NOT GET WORSE.** Spearman(skill rank, points rank) over the whole
   professional population **≥ 0.888**, and mean |skill − points| **≤ 10.1% of the table**.
   ⚠ The absolute-places form of the second half is **dropped as a bar and kept as a reported
   number**: my predecessor measured it as not scale-free (35.6 → 52.7 places while the share held at
   9.8% → 10.1%) and said so against its own interest. This wave triples the table, so the places
   form would fail by arithmetic alone and would mean nothing. The share is the scale-free form.
2. **A CHANCE, NOT A CONVEYOR – AND NOT A GIFT.** Three limbs.
   * **(a) THE CONVEYOR GUARD.** Of 180 careers, **fewer than 30 (17%)** ever reach the top 100 on
     **either** policy arm. Two careers in three becoming top-100 players is the failure mode that
     killed the last attempt.
   * **(b) THE WALL GUARD.** **At least 1** of 180 reaches the top 200 on each arm.
   * **(c) THE GIFT GUARD, RE-DENOMINATED.** `points-by-the-book` §4 recorded that its own gift guard
     ("LIVE girls inside the merged top 200 < 40") **read 0.0 on the baseline too and therefore had
     no dynamic range**, and asked the next wave to denominate it in the number that does: **LIVE
     girls holding a professional ranking at career end**, measured at 21 → 22 → 12 → 14 across that
     wave's arms. So: that median must not exceed **21** (a 50% rise on 14), **and** her median
     career rank improving at the same time as that count rises is a deflation and fails this
     criterion whatever it does to her.
   The full distribution is reported on every arm whatever the counts are – the record alone is how
   the last attempt failed to notice.
3. **THE REAL-CURVE CALIBRATION SURVIVES, AND THE APPEND PROPERTY WITH IT.** #10 / #50 / #100 /
   #150 / #250 / #300 each stay within **±40%** of the real anchors (4,000 / 1,400 / 850 / 520 /
   260 / 190) – the band `tests/season/fieldPros.test.ts` already enforces and `act2-pro-tour.md` §11
   calls a deliberate achievement. **And the stronger form this wave can promise: the top 520 rows of
   the merged table are byte-identical**, because a storey appended below shifts nobody's id. If that
   is not achievable it is reported as a loss, in those words.
4. **THE DOORS STILL SEPARATE PEOPLE.** The number of INERT acceptance cuts (past the pointed rows,
   refusing nobody) **does not rise above 2**, and no two adjacent cuts end up within **50 points** of
   each other. Two are inert today (w35 #700, w50 #550) and the arithmetic says a 1,600-row table
   should make both bite; that is a prediction in §2, not a criterion.
5. **THE TAPER IS A TAPER.** Measured for the player defined in §1c – *her, at the rung the engine's
   own scheduler puts her on* – and never for a fixed hypothetical alone. Four limbs:
   * **(a)** W15 lands in **15–35%**. This is `fieldPros.ts`' own shipped calibrated band and the one
     number both previous attempts broke (step 2: 3.5%; the §3d probe: 12.6%). Non-negotiable.
   * **(b)** The title chance is **monotone non-increasing** from W15 to Slam. A ladder whose middle
     is easier than its bottom is not a ladder.
   * **(c)** **At least 8 of the 10** rungs land inside their approved band.
   * **(d)** The Slam is **below 2%** – "an event of a career", read as at most one in a
     fifty-Slam career.
6. **COST, AND THE LEDGER.** `fieldProsFor` stays under **6 ms** per season boundary (3.08× the
   population at 1.58 ms is a 4.9 ms arithmetic expectation; 6 is the budget), and **the persisted
   ledger does not grow at all**. A field pro writes no result row and no save byte, so this is
   structural rather than hopeful – but if any persisted field moves, the three-part move applies
   (`SAVE_SCHEMA_VERSION` v41 → **v42**, append-only migration, golden fixture) and the wave says so
   in as many words.

Two things are explicitly **NOT** criteria, and saying so is the point:

* **Her peak rank on its own.** A wave that floats her into the top 100 by extending the table under
  everybody is the gift the brief names. Her rank is reported; criteria 1 and 2 are what judge it.
* **The R1/R2 exit rate falling.** A 32-draw exits 75% of any field by the second match by pure
  arithmetic (`real-ladder-pace.md` §2a). Reported against the real-world figures – 75.0% for a
  journeywoman, 55% for a future top-100 at ITF level, 76.5% for a world #47 – and never optimised.

### 0a. WHAT IS BEING MEASURED, AND FOR WHOM – criterion 5's own definition, fixed in advance

Criterion 5 is the only one whose *subject* is a choice, so the choice is made here rather than after
the numbers. Three candidates were considered and two were rejected **before any measurement**:

* ✗ **A fixed reference build at every rung.** `tools/field-quality.ts`' reference strong junior
  (power 56.75) is the shipped instrument and the origin of the 15–35% band, but she is one player
  measured against ten fields of rising strength: her taper is guaranteed by construction and says
  nothing about whether the rung *she stands on* is winnable. She is reported as a continuity column
  and is not the criterion.
* ✗ **The player at a rung's acceptance cut.** Precise, and wrong at both ends: `acceptsRank` and
  `entrantPctBand` are two different questions (`calendar.ts` says so outright), so a player at
  W35's #700 cut is the weakest entrant in a field drawn from #333, while a player at the Slam's #104
  cut meets a field drawn from #1.
* ✓ **THE RUNG THE ENGINE'S OWN SCHEDULER PUTS HER ON.** `tools/points-economy.ts` already computes,
  for a player of core C, the fixed point of *book → rank → window → book* and then the season a
  rational player of that strength would actually enter (`earnSeason`, greedy on expected value over
  the real calendar with one entry a week and the age cap). **Her own rung is the rung that season
  plays most often.** That maps every core to exactly one rung and every rung to a span of cores, so
  "the title chance at rung X" is asked of the player the ladder has actually placed there.

**The reported number for rung X is therefore `P(title) = p(C(X), F(X))^5`**, where `C(X)` is the
midpoint of the core span whose modal rung is X, `F(X)` is the mean core of the field
`selectEntrants` really draws at that rung, and 5 = log2(32), every rung in this game having a draw
of 32. The closed form is used because it is the same one `expectedPoints` already prices seasons
with, and because it **reproduces the real-tournament instrument to a tenth of a point**: at
`ladder-pace` step 1, field core 47.1 vs power 56.75 gives P(match) 75.5% and 0.755^5 = **24.5%**,
which is the number `field-quality.ts` measured by running the brackets.

**A rung the scheduler never chooses is reported as such and scored as absent**, not given a made-up
occupant.

### 0b. THE WEAKNESSES IN THIS RULE, STATED NOW RATHER THAN EXPLOITED LATER

My predecessor found its anti-gift criterion had no dynamic range and said so; three of these are the
same kind of admission.

1. **Criterion 5(c) is a count I chose, and I chose 8 of 10 rather than 10 of 10 because two of the
   ten rungs have never been entered by any career in either bench** (`wta500` 0/180, `wta1000`
   0/180, `slam` 0/180 at this head). A band for a rung nobody reaches is an extrapolation, and a
   criterion that demanded it would be demanding fiction.
2. **Criterion 5 is a target the owner set and a knob I control.** Unlike criteria 1–4 it can be hit
   by tuning the thing being measured. Its defence is 5(b) (monotone) and criteria 1, 2 and 4, which
   are what stop a taper being bought by deflating the world – and the fact that the taper is
   measured through `selectEntrants` and the real match engine rather than asserted.
3. **Criterion 2(c) still has a hole, and it is the mirror of the one it fixes.** A deeper table can
   leave the live count flat while lifting her alone; the second limb ("her median improving while
   the count rises") catches the deflation direction but not a pure gift to the kid. Criterion 1 is
   what covers that case, and the sentence is written here so the next wave does not have to
   rediscover it.
4. **Criterion 6's cost budget is generous on purpose.** A season boundary happens once a year of
   game time; 6 ms is invisible. The number is reported because the brief asked for it, not because
   it is close to binding.

## 0c. THE TWO INSTRUMENTS DISAGREED ABOUT `FIELD`, AND THE DISAGREEMENT IS THE ANSWER

CLAUDE.md's rule is that when the graph and grep disagree you check the grep first, because in both
recorded disputes the graph was right and the search was broken. This time **neither is broken and
they are answering different questions**, which is worth recording as a third outcome.

* `graphify explain "FIELD"` returns **six edges** – five importers plus the `contains` edge to its
  own file.
* `git grep -l "engine/season/fieldPros" -- tests/` returns **seven test files**.

The graph node is the **constant**; the grep is the **module**. Five files import `FIELD` itself
(`tests/season/fieldPros.test.ts`, `tests/unranked-sentinel.test.ts`, `tools/points-economy.ts`,
`tools/skill-ceiling.ts`, `tools/world-turnover.ts` – verified with
`git grep -ln "{ FIELD\|FIELD }"`, which returns exactly those five); the other five test files
import `fieldProsFor` / `mergedWtaRanking` / `universeForTier` and never touch the constant. **Both
tools were right and the blast radius is the union, not either one** – and it is the union that
mattered here, because taking `FIELD.size` to 1,600 moves ranks, which moves what
`fieldProsFor`'s callers see.

## 1. STEP A – THE POPULATION IS THE SPORT'S (`FIELD.size` 520 → 1,600)

**What changed, in one file and nowhere else.** Three storeys of 360 – `qualifier`, `satellite`,
`newcomer` – **appended** below `circuit`. `fieldProsFor` walks `FIELD.tiers` in order handing out
`fp-<n>`, so appending shifts nobody's id: **`fp-0`…`fp-519` keep their chairs, their storeys, their
names, their books and their careers byte-for-byte.** The append trick that made 364 → 520 safe made
520 → 1,600 safe too, and the receipt is in §1b P1.

**The points bands are the real curve's own tail**, log-interpolated from the seam our table already
holds – (#520, 70), which is `circuit`'s floor – to the real list's own last row (#1,531 on 3 points,
`real-ladder-pace.md` §5). That gives #700 ≈ 41 · #1,000 ≈ 17 · #1,300 ≈ 7 · #1,600 = 3, which sits
between our own deliberately-mean tail anchors and the live 2026 curve (#700 = 59, #1,000 = 22) –
i.e. it errs in the direction §5 of that page says our anchors were mean. Every seam is continuous by
construction: each storey's ceiling **is** the floor of the storey above.

**Ageing, retirement and succession are untouched and cover the new chairs for free.** `careerAt` is
a walk keyed on `(seed, n, season)` off `seed:fieldcareer:`; `n` is simply larger. No schema, no
migration, no golden save, no MAIN draw, zero persisted bytes –
`docs/specs/world-strength-audit-2026-08.md` §9 is preserved entire.

### 1a. ⚠⚠ THE FIRST CUT OF THE CORE BANDS WAS MEASURED AND WRONG, AND IT IS KEPT

The core bands were the one judgement in step A, and I got them wrong first. The reasoning was
straightforward and sounded right: the pyramid's rule is "down five, spanning ten", continued four
more times it reaches core 13 – below anything the match engine can call a tennis player – so derive
the tail from the real Elo curve's own gradient instead (~250 Elo per decade of rank, so #520 →
#1,600 is ~122 Elo ≈ 6 core points at our measured 20.2 Elo per core point). Four storeys stepping
down **two**: 31–41 / 29–39 / 27–37 / 25–35.

**Measured, it failed ship criterion 1 outright: Spearman 0.888 → 0.818, mean gap 10.1% → 12.8% of
the table.** And the arithmetic says exactly why. Points inside a storey are a monotone function of
the drawn core, so every skill/points inversion in the whole table comes from the **overlap between
adjacent storeys**. For two uniform bands of width 10 offset by 5, the chance the lower storey
out-draws the higher one is 12.5%; offset by 2 it is **32%**. Squeezing 1,080 professionals – two
thirds of the population – into sixteen core points destroyed the correspondence.

> **THE FIFTY-PER-CENT OVERLAP IS NOT DECORATION. IT IS WHAT THE CORRESPONDENCE IS MADE OF.** This is
> not written anywhere in the file the rule lives in, and it is now.

Restored to the pyramid's own arithmetic – three storeys of 360, cores 28–38 / 23–33 / 18–28 –
**Spearman went 0.888 → 0.930 and the mean gap 10.1% → 8.1% of the table.** Both better than the
baseline, which is the opposite of what a wave that triples the population would be expected to do.

⚠ **And yes, that makes the world more spread out again, exactly as the fifth storey's note said it
would and for the same reason: a step must be DEPTH and nothing else or its effect is not
attributable.** Compressing the strength scale while deepening the table would have made the two
inseparable. The compression is §3, on its own arm.
