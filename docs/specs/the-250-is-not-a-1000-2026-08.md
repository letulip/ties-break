wild-card proposal that is **costed and NOT built**, which is the owner's own instruction.

match model gives P(QF+) = 15.0% at a 250, and her two-season drought is a 20.5% event. **The defect

## 1. THE PREDICTIONS, WRITTEN BEFORE THE RUN

Committed in `0d35f3d` before a single measurement, so the column below is a prediction and not a
description. **The storey arithmetic they come from**, since a prediction with no derivation is a

consecutive positions** from the head of its band – which is why the shipped 250's window `#32-#82`
predicts a field core of `(33x72 + 18x61) / 51 = 68.1` against the **68.4 measured**. The model works,
so it is used to predict the sweep.

⭐ **The field-core column is right and the expected-points column is systematically wrong in ONE
direction, which is worth recording because it is the more useful of the two facts.** The storey model
predicts *who is in the draw* to within 2-5 core points at every setting; it over-predicts what
weakening the field is *worth* to her, at every setting, by 40-70%. The reason is that expected points
at a 32-draw are dominated by five consecutive matches, and a bracket does not pay linearly in the
mean core of the field it contains – the tail of strong opponents she can still meet in round four
matters more than the mean says. **So a field-strength model is a good instrument for composition and
a bad one for outcome, and this file is the receipt for saying so.**

**The criteria were §1's, written first**: (1) the 250 must stop being the worst rung – it must pay

250 *still* below the W50, which does not fix the sentence he actually wrote; #80 makes a WTA 250 the
best event in the game for a #121 player, which is a worse defect than the one being fixed.

⭐ **AND #64 IS NOT A ROUND NUMBER – IT IS `FIELD.tiers[0].count`**, the *tourElite* storey exactly.
So the rule states a fact about the world rather than a tuning constant: **the top storey does not play
a WTA 250.** Against the sport it lands where the one sourced play-down rule we already ship lands –
the ITF bars WTA #1-50 from every W-series event and `PLAY_DOWN.fromAllW` is 50 – and against the real
points curve #64 is about the real #55-60.

⚠ **The criterion I dropped, and why, because it was in the pre-registration and it should not
disappear quietly.** §1 also asked that a 250 pay more than a **WTA 125 (33.5)**. It does not quite –
33.3 against 33.5 – and on reflection **the criterion was wrong**, not the result. A WTA 125 series
exists in the real sport *precisely* so that a player ranked #100-#150 has a rung where she is the
favourite; her best expected value there rather than at a 250 is the ladder working, not failing. The
criterion that survives is the owner's own sentence, and the 250 clears it.

⚠ **Sampling noise, stated so the table is not over-read.** The point distribution at a 250 is heavy
(a title is 250 points at ~2%), so the standard error on "exp pts" is about **±1.1 at n = 1200** and
±2.1 at n = 300. Everything above is separated by more than that except the 250-vs-125 comparison in
the previous paragraph, which is why that one is argued rather than asserted.

### 5e. ⭐⭐ THE RECONCILIATION – A CONTRADICTING MEASUREMENT, AND WHAT IT CHANGED

A coordinator's arm (`tools/big-rung-finishes.ts`, `f2f8b18` – the **observed ledger** over 54 driven
careers rather than a replay) reported its output **byte-identical before and after this change**, and
`ladder-baseline --seeds 6` on the shipped tree at **career high median #110** against my #94. Both
claims are reproducible and neither was wrong to make. Four runs settled it, and **each of us was
measuring something the other was not.**

**1. The seed conventions are the same.** Both tools index `openCareer(preset, s, POLICIES[1])`, so
`--seeds 6` is a strict subset of `--seeds 10`. Not the explanation. **And #110 reproduces exactly**:
the shipped tree at n = 54 gives #110, so that number is right and mine was right too – they are the
same effect read at two sample sizes, and §5's table now prints both.

**2. The gate is NOT inert, and the suspected fallback never fires.** `selectEntrants` instrumented
over one full driven career (676 weeks), counting `wta250` calls:

| call site | calls | mean band | mean gated | calls where the head removed somebody |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **her own draw** (shadow) | **70** | 376.3 | 357.6 | **63 / 70** |
| the canonical AI bracket | 104 | 399.0 | 367.0 | **104 / 104** |

⭐ **174 of 174 apply the gate; the `gated.length >= drawSize` fallback fires ZERO times** – the band
holds ~390 candidates against a draw of 32, so it has 350 to spare. The suspicion that the rule is
inert by construction at 64 is **disproved**.

**3. ⚠⚠ AND MY OWN CHECK OF THAT PRODUCED A FALSE NULL FIRST, WHICH IS THE METHODOLOGICAL FINDING OF
THE DAY.** My first "250-only" arm put `acceptsFromRank: 64` into `calendar.ts` **in a worktree at
`0d35f3d`** – the commit *before* the engine change – where `selectEntrants` has no code that reads
the field. It produced byte-identical output, exactly as it must, and I nearly reported it as
"the constant does nothing". **A constant added to a tree without its reader is a null arm that looks
like a null result.** The fix is one line of hygiene: an A/B arm must assert that the mechanism it is
toggling is *present*, not merely that the number is set.

**4. THE DECOMPOSED TRUTH, on the coordinator's own instrument and population (n = 54).** Two changes
shipped together, so all four cells were run – the 250-only arm on a tree that HAS the reader:

| arm | 250 entries | ever past R1 | ever QF+ | ever final+ | ever title |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| pre-change | 250 | 40 (81.6%) | 33 (67.3%) | 15 (30.6%) | 10 (20.4%) |
| slam-only | 254 | 40 (81.6%) | 33 (67.3%) | 14 (28.6%) | 10 (20.4%) |
| ⭐ **250-only** | **273** | **44 (88.0%)** | **36 (72.0%)** | 12 (24.0%) | 9 (18.0%) |
| both (shipped) | 267 | 44 (88.0%) | 35 (70.0%) | 11 (22.0%) | 9 (18.0%) |

**So the head moves that instrument substantially, and the reported byte-identity cannot be a property
of the change.** The likeliest cause is the same family of error as mine in (3): one of the two arms
was not the tree it was labelled. That is a provenance failure, not a measurement failure, and I hit
it myself in the same hour.

**5. ⚠⚠ AND THE RESULT IS MIXED, WHICH THE REPLAY DID NOT SHOW.** Per ENTRY at a WTA 250, 250-only
against pre-change:

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

### 5d. ⚠⚠ THE SYMMETRIC HALF, DELIBERATELY NOT TAKEN – AND IT IS THE OWNER'S TO RULE ON

**Nothing stops HER entering a WTA 250 once she is inside #64.** The rule shipped here is read by
`selectEntrants` and by nothing else, so the field obeys a head that the player does not. That is an
asymmetry and it is deliberate; here is the case both ways, because it is his call and not an agent's.

**FOR taking it.** It is one rule instead of two, the machinery already exists (`playDownBars`, a live
rank READ that self-reverses the week she drops back – the owner named that property himself on
15.08), and the codebase's own standard for this family is *"she is accepted if she would be inside
the field they draw from"*. Leaving the two halves disagreeing is exactly the shape `rank-plateau.md`
§2b was written about.

**AGAINST, and it is why it is not taken today.** Three reasons, in order of weight:

1. ⭐ **It REMOVES content from a player.** Every refusal in this engine carries the promise «игрок
   должен иметь возможность играть… чтобы не скучал». Gating the field ADDS her opportunity; gating
   her takes one away. Doing both in one wave, in answer to a complaint that she *cannot move*, would
   have shipped the fix and its own consolation prize together.
2. **The number is not sourced for HER.** `PLAY_DOWN.fromAllW = 50` quotes a real regulation the ITF
   publishes. The WTA publishes **no** acceptance head for a 250 – only the commitment system – so
   #64 is a model of a scheduling pressure. Modelling a pressure on the field is fair; refusing the
   player at a door the rulebook does not close is a larger claim.
3. **It is unreachable in the measured population and therefore cannot be measured yet.** n = 90,
   career high: best **#9**, p25 **#48**, median #94. A cut at #64 would bite about a quarter of
   careers and only near their peak, so its real cost is a tail question that wants its own bench
   rather than a paragraph.

⚠ **WHAT IT WOULD COST WHEN HE WANTS IT** – so it is not re-derived: `playDownBars` gains a per-rung
WTA arm reading the same `TIERS[tier].acceptsFromRank`, `playDownRefusalDetail` gains the sentence,
`isWSeriesTier`'s scope fence widens, and `tools/boredom-guard.ts` re-runs to prove the promise in (1)
still holds. **Small, and none of it is guessed.**

## 7. THE WILD CARDS – SHAPED AND COSTED, **NOT BUILT**

**«надо подумать про wild card 8».** Eight of a Slam's 128 places are given by the tournament rather
than earned by ranking, and we model neither them nor the qualifiers. This section answers the design
questions and stops. ⚠ **Nothing here is implemented and no constant for it exists.**

### 7a. THE FRAME, FIRST, BECAUSE IT CHANGES WHAT THE FEATURE IS FOR

⚠ **A wild card is not a fix for a stall, because there is no stall.** §6 measures **0 of 14 careers**
whose career best sits inside the refused band, at 104 and at 112 alike, and after 112 the median
career is refused **14 weeks**. So this buys a **story** – "she got the wild card" – and not progress.
That is the honest frame for every ruling below, and it is the opposite of the frame item 4 had.

### 7b. RULING ONE – WHO GETS ONE. Reality uses three grounds; we can express two.

| ground | can our engine say it? | what it needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **a home player** | ⚠ not yet – **an event has no country** | a host nation per event. There is no venue or city machinery anywhere in `src/` (checked). But `AiPlayer.nation` exists (ISO-2, `NATION_POOL`), field pros carry one, and her profile carries a country – so once an event has a nation this is one comparison |
| **a young prospect** | ✅ free | `AiPlayer.ageYears` exists; `juniorReservedPlace` is already this idea one ladder down and is the model to copy |
| **a returning name** | ❌ **not expressible – drop it explicitly** | it needs a player with a remembered past (a former high rank, an absence). Field pros persist **zero bytes** – `careerAt` walks a succession from (seed, n) – so "she used to be #12 and has been away" has nowhere to live. Half-building it would mean inventing a history at read time |

### 7c. RULING TWO – CAN SHE GET ONE, AND DOES IT READ AS A GIFT OR A REWARD

Three shapes, and the difference is entirely *where the card comes from*:

| | reads as | can it be farmed? | cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ⭐ **(a) home-nation card** – hers at the major whose host nation matches her profile country | **a reward for being from somewhere**, which is what it is in the sport | **no – naturally capped**: at most one major a season can be her home major | **lowest** |
| (b) form card – a threshold she can hit (a title at the rung below inside N weeks) | a reward | yes, and it is a second acceptance rule with its own tuning | medium |
| (c) random card | ⚠ **a gift** – and it will be read as the engine deciding her career for her | n/a | low to build, high to regret |

**The recommendation, and it is a proposal and not a decision: (a).** It is the only one with a cap
built into its own definition, it is the only one that needs no new balance number, and it is the one
that makes the marker he asked for mean something – *"wild card · home nation"* says why.

### 7d. RULING THREE – WHAT THE CARD SAYS

Two surfaces, and one of them is blocked this week:

* the **tournament card** – a badge on the event row in `SeasonScreen.vue`. ⚠⚠ **Another agent is
  working in that file right now**, which is a second, independent reason this wave does not build it.

## 8. HIS SECOND CLAUSE – «на нижних вылетала периодически»

**Measured, and the answer is no: the field-strength shape does NOT reach below the WTA 125, and her
early exits down there are exactly what her own build predicts.**

**The field she meets, rung by rung** – the shape is smooth all the way up to the 125 and then falls
off a cliff, which is the whole finding of item 4 seen from underneath:

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| field core | 44.6 | 46.5 | 50.1 | 55.0 | **68.4** |
| % stronger than her | 5% | 7% | 2% | 32% | **97%** |

**Her actual record, off the counting window in her save** (`tools/two-seasons-read.ts` – the ledger
prunes to a rolling 52 weeks, so this is season 8, 9 events below the WTA 125):

| rung | events | finishes | exits before the QF |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| w50 | 4 | CHAMPION x3, quarter | **0** |
| w75 | 3 | CHAMPION, **R16, R16** | **2** |
| w100 | 2 | semi, quarter | **0** |
| | **9** | | **2** |

**What the model says to expect over those same nine events**: `4 x 18.8% + 3 x 22.3% + 2 x 25.0%` =
**1.92**. ⭐ **Observed 2.** A 32-draw is five rounds, so even a player who reaches the quarter-finals
78% of the time goes out early about one event in five – **"periodically" is the correct word and the
correct rate.**

⚠ **THE RECONSTRUCTION IS EXACT AND NOT AN ESTIMATE, which matters because "periodically" is a claim
about a count.** A 32-draw is a five-round fold, so one loss is one event: `4 events / 17W-1L` at W50
can only be three titles and one two-win run, and `3 events / 7W-2L` at W75 can only be a title and two
short ones. The counting window then NAMES all nine finishes – `434 · 446 · 454 CHAMPION`, `429
quarter`, `443 CHAMPION`, `461 R16`, `424 R16`, `457 semi`, `432 quarter` – and they reconcile with the
W-L totals exactly.

⭐⭐ **AND THE DETAIL THAT SETTLES IT: both of those exits were R16 – she WON her opening match and
lost the second.** Below the WTA 125 she did not lose a single opening match all season. ⚠ **She did
lose one first round: her one WTA 250, for 1 point** – which is item 4, from the other side, and is
the one event of the twenty-five that this wave changes. **There is no second defect underneath it.**
