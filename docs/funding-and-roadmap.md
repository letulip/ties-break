# Ties Break – Funding Routes, Roadmap & Cost Model (August 2026)

## Current truth

How products like this attract money globally, what each route costs and requires, what the market evidence says a tennis-parent sim can earn, and a phased roadmap with investment estimates from "scrappy solo" to "funded studio". Figures are ranges with sources and data years; anything unsourced is marked as an estimate.

## Verdict

**This is a fundable project but not a venture-scale one, and the cheapest mistake to avoid is raising money before manufacturing evidence.** The realistic capital need to a credible commercial 1.0 is small – **~$13–15k scrappy floor, ~$40–45k cash base case (~$60–70k with a modest founder draw for 7–9 months), ~$105k high** – because the solo+AI pipeline has already demonstrated 10–30× conventional build velocity (544 commits in 10 days) and the remaining brake is design decisions, not hands. At that size, the routes that fit are: **self-funding, web-portal soft launch as a free evidence engine, a non-dilutive NZ grant if relocation happens, and a small milestone-framed publisher or project-fund deal ($30–120k ask) once the loop is closed and portal data exists.** Games VC is off-thesis for a single premium title and should not be prepared for. The market evidence brackets outcomes honestly: P50 across channels ≈ **$30–95k gross over 18 months**; P90 ≈ **$300–700k** (the Football Coach: College Dynasty scenario); the tail above that exists (Volcano Princess: two people, 1.5M+ copies) but nobody can underwrite it. Two things must happen before ANY route is approached: the P7 legal wave (the repo is legally floating today) and P1+P2 (a career that can end and a child who is a person – without them the pitch's payoff moment doesn't exist in the build).

**Do first, in order: (1) P7 legal wave + name-clearance search, (2) P1+P2, (3) CrazyGames soft launch for 4–8 weeks of dashboard data, (4) then choose between staying independent, the NZ grant track, and a small publisher/project-fund conversation with data in hand.**

## Part I – The routes (how such products attract money)

### Route 1: Indie game publishers

**How it works.** Publisher funds development (advance) and/or marketing for a revenue share after recouping the advance. Sourced via pitch decks to submission portals, scouts watching Steam/festivals, and GDC/Gamescom meetings; warm intros materially outperform cold inboxes ([IndieGameBusiness, 2025](https://indiegamebusiness.com/state-of-pitching-in-2025/)). Scouts do find nobodies – Balatro's solo dev was cold-DM'd by a Playstack scout in 2023 pre-hype ([localthunk's timeline](https://localthunk.com/blog/balatro-timeline-3aarh)).

**The money (published data).** Voyer Law's 2025 survey of indie publishing agreements: **median advance $300k, average $674k, range $20k–$6M+; dev revenue share averages 58% with an advance (median 50%) and 68% without (median 70%); 81% of deals recoup before full payouts** ([GameDiscoverCo write-up](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-should-a-game-publishing-agreement)). Climate caveat: publishing money got sharply scarcer after 2023; for a niche sim from an unproven-in-genre solo dev, the realistic 2025–26 offer is low-six-figure or no-advance/marketing-only (estimate). IP normally stays with the developer.

**Who fits this game.** Hooded Horse is the genre gold standard and publishes its terms: **dev keeps 65% from dollar one, no recoup, ≥$100k localization+marketing commitment per title** ([MCV interview, 2023](https://mcvuk.com/business-news/harnessing-hooded-horse-power-tim-bender-and-snow-rui-on-their-indie-publisher/)). Also relevant: Yogscast Games (built for teams ≤5, half their releases are first-time devs), Playstack (solo-dev proven), Slitherine (niche-sim audience, off-thesis genre), Paradox Arc, tinyBuild, Team17, Raw Fury. Raw Fury signs <1% of 1,500+ yearly pitches ([GWO, 2021](https://gameworldobserver.com/2021/10/19/raw-fury-says-it-receives-over-1500-pitches-year-and-signs-less-than-1-of-those)) – festivals and scouts beat cold email.

**Timeline.** First contact → signed: 2–6 months typical, year-long outliers; signed → launch adds 12–24 months ([FirstLook guide](https://firstlook.gg/blog/how-to-get-indie-game-published/)).

**Prerequisites for Ties Break.** A Steam page + demo + deck – a web-only PWA is invisible to this route today. Genre white space is real: no publisher-signed tennis/sports-management indie was found in 2024–26 – no comps, but also no proven demand at publishers.

### Route 2: Project-finance funds (the best-fit institutional money)

A 2024–26 growth area sitting between publishers and VC: **revenue-share project financing, no equity, dev keeps IP and control.**

- **Outersloth** (Innersloth's $25M fund): checks **$50k–$2M**, published contract: 50/50 split until 1× recoup, then 15% to Outersloth, no publishing obligations; **13 of its first 22 funded games had budgets under $500k** – genuinely solo-compatible, open pitches ([the contract, 2026](https://www.innersloth.com/the-outersloth-contract/)).
- **Griffin Special Opportunities Fund** (May 2026): **$100M explicitly non-equity project financing for indie games**, hundreds of thousands to low millions per deal – run by Hooded Horse's CEO Tim Bender as MD ([GamesBeat, 2026](https://gamesbeat.com/griffin-gaming-partners-launches-100m-project-financing-fund-for-indie-games/)). The strongest 2026 signal that this class of money is institutionalizing, and the person reading the pitch understands management sims.
- **Kowloon Nights**: typically $500k–$5M budgets – floor likely above this project's need today; 2–4 weeks from internal yes to contract.
- **WINGS Interactive**: up to $500k on the same 50/50→15% shape, but requires women/marginalized-gender devs in key roles – not eligible as a solo male founder.

**Fit: high.** Best terms in the market for exactly this class of niche, well-crafted title. Same prerequisites as publishers (evidence + materials), smaller asks welcome.

### Route 3: Games VC, pre-seed and accelerators – explicitly not recommended

Fund math needs outlier compounding returns; premium single games are hit-driven one-shots, so VCs fund platforms, tools, live-service and repeatable-engine studios instead ([Naavik, 2024](https://naavik.co/digest/building-game-studios-venture-capital-runs-dry/)). The climate is a funding winter: gaming VC was **$373M in Q1 2025 (–41% YoY) and $193M in Q2**, vs $10.2B in 2021 ([Konvoy data](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/konvoy-releases-q1-2025-gaming-industry-report-stabilized-funding-environment-and-ais-emerging-role-302429810.html)). Reference points if ever relevant: Play Ventures $250k–2M, Sisu $250k–500k pre-seed, The Games Fund $500k–3M; a16z SPEEDRUN takes ~0.4% of applicants and gives $500k (+$500k follow-on) for 10%. **This route only opens if the ambition pivots to "the FM-of-tennis platform" with recurring revenue and a team – a different company, not this roadmap.** Time spent on VC prep today is time not spent shipping.

### Route 4: Web portals – zero money up front, but the evidence factory

The one route where the web-first PWA is an advantage, and the recommended **first** move regardless of which money route follows.

- **Terms (2025–26):** Poki – 50/50 rev share on portal traffic, **100% to the dev on own-brought traffic**; realistic first-web-game income **$500–3,000/month** ([IndieGameBusiness, 2026](https://indiegamebusiness.com/web-gaming-for-indie-developers/)). CrazyGames – no exclusivity, €100 payout threshold; its 2026 jam terms show 60% of ads / 70% of IAP to devs; 35–50M MAU ([docs](https://docs.crazygames.com/faq/); [GamesBeat, 2025](https://gamesbeat.com/crazygames-hits-35m-users-for-browser-games-and-launches-social-multiplayer-features/)). Yandex Games – 50M+ MAU, ads-dominant, RU-rails-friendly ([WN Hub, 2025](https://wnhub.io/news/other/item-49945)).
- **Why it matters more than the money:** the developer dashboard reports **players, D1 retention, playtime, conversion-to-gameplay – with zero self-built analytics**, preserving the game's genuine zero-telemetry differentiator. The portal's published bar: platform-average D1 6.7%, top titles convert 80%+ of visitors to ≥1 minute, 10+ min average playtime, <20 MB build, <10 s load ([CrazyGames metrics docs](https://docs.crazygames.com/resources/basic-launch-metrics/)). The ~140 KB app shell crushes the size bar; the risk metric is first-minute conversion (onboarding precedes the first match).
- **The funnel effect is quantified now:** **62% of web gamers have downloaded or purchased a game they discovered in the browser** ([Poki 2026 State of Web Gaming report](https://poki.com/blog/state-of-web-gaming-report-2026)) – the portal build is worth running primarily as a Steam-wishlist engine (Retro Bowl's free-web + paid-unlock template).
- **Fit warning:** a session-long reading-heavy premium sim is off the portal meta; the P90 portal outcome requires a purpose-built arcade-ized "junior season" cut, not the full sim. DesktopDrift pipeline experience makes this channel near-zero-cost to try.

Netflix Games and Apple Arcade – upfront-buyout routes – narrowed to kids/party/mainstream/licensed in 2025–26 and are out of thesis for a niche sim ([mobilegamer.biz, 2024](https://mobilegamer.biz/inside-apple-arcade-axed-games-declining-payouts-disillusioned-studios-and-an-uncertain-future/); [Forbes on Netflix's pivot, Nov 2025](https://www.forbes.com/sites/dbloom/2025/11/13/netflix-showcases-new-video-game-strategy-in-time-for-holidays/)).

### Route 5: Crowdfunding – a marketing asset, not a budget

Kickstarter 2024: 441 successful video-game campaigns, $26M total – **average ≈$59k per successful campaign**, category success rate ~32%, and the channel is structurally smaller than its 2013 peak ([Kickstarter, 2025](https://updates.kickstarter.com/kickstarter-biggest-platform-for-games/); [GameDevReports, 2025](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/kickstarter-in-2025-and-before)). No successful tennis/sports-management video-game campaign was found in 2024–26. Net after fees/rewards: ~80–85% of gross. Real value: proof of audience, email list, wishlist push, diligence ammunition. **Hard constraint: Kickstarter requires residency/entity in a supported country – Russia is not on the list; New Zealand is** ([eligibility](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005128594)). This route opens post-relocation, sized $20–60k, best timed to the Steam-page launch.

### Route 6: Grants and non-dilutive public money (location-dependent)

- **New Zealand (the live option):** CODE's current tiers – **KickStart NZ$10k–60k** for prototypes with a functioning core loop (this game qualifies on substance today), **StartUp NZ$60k–250k**; annual rounds, EOI-to-money roughly half a year; government doubled CODE's budget from 2026 (+NZ$2.75M/yr) ([nz-code.nz/funding](https://www.nz-code.nz/funding); [Beehive, 2025](https://www.beehive.govt.nz/release/govt-increases-support-thriving-games-industry)). The 20% Game Development Sector Rebate needs NZ$250k+ annual eligible spend – relevant only at T2 scale ([NZ On Air](https://www.nzonair.govt.nz/funding/game-development-sector-rebate-scheme-gdsr/)). NZ's game industry grew 38.6% to NZ$759.6M in 2024/25 – the ecosystem is real ([NZGDA](https://www.nzgda.com/blog/new-zealand-game-development-industry-breaks-records)).
- **Elsewhere, all requiring local entity/residency:** UK Games Fund Prototype £30k (rising to £100k from July 2026); EU Creative Europe up to €200k at ≤60% of costs (needs an EU entity with a commercially released game – DesktopDrift arguably satisfies the track-record clause in substance); Canada Media Fund up to C$1.5M at 75%; Germany's federal program reopened Aug 2025 with €125M/yr earmarked from 2026. Epic MegaGrants: ineligible (not Unreal).
- **RU-origin practicalities (documented only):** the gates are residency and entity, not nationality; sanctions-era KYC makes clean structure matter; startups with Russian-diaspora founders still raised $3B+ in 2025 ([Oninvest, 2026](https://en.oninvest.com/article/startups-with-russian-roots-have-survived-one-crisis-but-there-is-a-new-one-ahead)) – a hurdle, not a wall, once relocated.

**If the NZ move happens, CODE KickStart is the best money-for-effort in this entire document** – non-dilutive, sized exactly for finishing T1, and it converts the relocation from a funding obstacle into the unlock.

### Route 7: Self-funding (the default), plus revenue-based financing later

The sober anchor: the median Steam indie earns $5–15k lifetime while the top 5% clear $1M ([compilation, 2026](https://rec0ded88.com/statistics/indie-game-sales/)). Consulting-subsidized development plus portal trickle revenue preserves 100% IP and all optionality; its cost is calendar time. Revenue-based credit (Pollen-class, up to 4× monthly revenue) only becomes available once revenue exists – a post-launch tool, not a path to 1.0.

## Part II – What the market evidence says (comparables)

**The direct market is tiny.** The entire visible PC tennis-management spend is ~$100–300k/yr across all titles (Boxleiter estimates from same-day Steam API pulls, 2026-08-01): Tennis Manager 2024 – 119 lifetime reviews (≈$60–130k gross) despite a ~16-person studio and Mouratoglou branding; TM25 went Mixed (69%); Tennis Elbow Manager 2 – ≈$116k in 8 years. Even licensed AAA (TopSpin 2K25) sits at Mixed. Tennis media underperforms too (Netflix cancelled Break Point on weak viewership).

**The adjacent paths are proven at exactly this team size.**
- **Football Coach: College Dynasty** – reported solo dev, license-free fictional teams, $19.99, Early Access 2024: **1,489 reviews, 96% positive, ≈$564k gross est.** ([estimator](https://steam-revenue-calculator.com/app/2151290/football-coach:-college-dynasty)). The realistic best-plausible-path comp.
- **Parent-raising premise:** Chinese Parents – 2.3M+ copies; **Volcano Princess – a 2-person team, 1.5M+ copies by 2026, ≈$9–13M gross est.** ([Steam news](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1669980/view/3686807969124122626); [ixbt, 2026](https://ixbt.games/en/news/2026/07/18/volcano-princess-s-97-polozitelnyx-otzyvov-v-steam-vyidet-na-playstation-5-nintendo-switch-i-smartfonax-30-iiulia.html)). Both rode a China-market parenting nerve; no Western sports-parenting equivalent has been tried – open whitespace, genuinely unproven.
- **Accessible-sports-sim ceiling:** Retro Bowl – 40M+ downloads, $1 unlock, free web version on portals, trajectory ended in an NFL license and Apple Arcade exclusivity.

**Steam physics for planning (2024–25 data):** median self-published indie made $3,285 (filtered set); #1,000 of the year ≈ $118k; wishlist→week-1 sales converts at 0.10–0.15×; Next Fest median demo gains <500 wishlists (top 5% ≈ 7,000); the $20–29.99 price band outperforms lower bands among games that sell ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/the-shape-of-the-pc-new-game-market); [wishlist study](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/the-state-of-steam-wishlist-conversions)). Audience macro is at all-time highs (US participation 27.3M record 2025; 106M global; WTA audience 1.1B record 2024) – but participation demonstrably does not convert to tennis-game purchases; the hook has to carry discovery, and fictional names forfeit the search traffic that carries FM/OOTP.

**Honest revenue brackets for THIS game** (estimates, gross, first ~18 months per channel, assuming P1+P2 land first – without endings/morale, halve the P50s):

| Channel | P25 | P50 | P90 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steam premium ($17.99–19.99, demo + Next Fest) | $3–15k | $25–80k | $250–600k (the FCCD scenario) |
| Web portals (free/cut-down build) | ~$0–1k | $2–10k/yr | $25–60k/yr (needs an arcade-ized spin-off + featuring) |
| Mobile (TWA $4.99–6.99 unlock; iOS later) | <$500/yr | $2–10k/yr | $30–80k/yr (Pocket-GM-class compounding, 2–3 yrs) |
| **Portfolio** | | **$30–95k / 18 mo** | **$300–700k** (~5–10% likelihood, conditional) |

**For investability:** only title combining two proven lanes (license-free solo sports-management × parent-raising premise) with the market's sole parent-POV angle; audience macro at records while the niche is supply-starved (competitive set = two small games, one dormant since 2018); the remaining bet is small and multi-channel (one codebase → portal, Steam, mobile). **Against:** direct-comp revenue is tiny; discovery math is stacked against license-free niche premium; monetization is structurally unresolved (owner decision: none at launch; portals pay groceries; premium needs Steam). Net: a strong small bet, a poor venture story – which is exactly what Part I's route ranking reflects.

## Part IV – Investor readiness (what must be true before pitching)

### The metrics bar (2025–26)

- **Portal/web:** CrazyGames platform-average D1 = 6.7%; top titles convert 80%+ of visitors to ≥1 min; 10+ min average playtime ([docs](https://docs.crazygames.com/resources/basic-launch-metrics/)).
- **Mobile-adjacent context:** median D1 ≈ 27%, D7 ≈ 3.4–3.9%, 75% of projects under 3% D28 (GameAnalytics end-2024 data) – a weekly-turn sim should behave closer to habitual genres (assessment). Pull the genre tables from GameAnalytics' 2026 report before any pitch.
- **Steam:** wishlist tiers as of June 2026 – 5k Bronze / 8k Silver / 50k Gold ([Zukowski](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-many-wishlists-before-launch)); conversion medians above.
- **Core-loop completion:** no industry benchmark exists – define the funnel (onboarding → first match watched → week 4 → season wrap → season 2) and report absolute cohort counts.

**Evidence without betraying zero-telemetry:** portal dashboards (the portal's data, not yours); opt-in minimal telemetry only if ever needed (never the child's name/birthday from saves); the deterministic ~100-byte replay format enables a "share my season" export – consent-by-action evidence almost no pitching indie has; structured playtest cohorts (20–50 testers, fixed builds, exported .tsave files).

**The license conflict, precisely:** PolyForm Shield constrains the public, not the owner – the owner retains full copyright and can grant a publisher exclusive commercial rights on top. Precedent: Barotrauma's source was public since 2017 and it still signed Daedalic in 2019. Resolution ladder: keep repo public as marketing → dual-license with a private commercial branch → private at signing (worst for the transparent-math brand). Chain-of-title hygiene: no CLA/DCO exists for accepted bug-fix PRs – adopt one or accept no third-party code; disclose AI-assisted authorship matter-of-factly.

**Name risk is real and specific:** "**TIEBREAK: Official Game of the ATP and WTA**" (Big Ant/Nacon, Aug 2024, PS/Xbox/PC) and "Tie Break Tens" (ITF-recognized event series) both exist. Search results for "ties break game" surface Nacon's title first; confusion/opposition risk if registered marks exist in games classes. **Commission a professional trademark clearance before spending marketing money on the name; "Ace Parent" is the distinctive element and the fallback primary mark.** Also: `cohort.ts` claims "no surname of a real professional" but the pool contains Pavic, Bondar, Yuan, Haas and others – legal exposure low (surname-only, fictional context; assessment, not legal advice), but a diligence grep finds the falsifiable claim in a minute; reword it and stop appending distinctive active-player surnames.

## The five ways, end to end

**Path 1 – Independent bootstrap (default, recommended baseline).** Phases A→D on the LOW/BASE columns: **$13–45k cash** over 7–12 months, 100% ownership, all upside, all risk. Choose it if: the NZ move stays uncertain and the P50 outcome ($30–95k/18 mo) is acceptable as a portfolio bet. First actions: P7, P1+P2, CrazyGames soft launch.

**Path 2 – Grant-assisted (NZ CODE track).** Same roadmap, but after relocation + NZ entity: CODE KickStart (NZ$10–60k) funds T1's cash lines non-dilutively; StartUp (NZ$60–250k) becomes reachable with portal/Steam evidence; Kickstarter also unlocks. Adds ~6 months of grant cadence; costs nothing but paperwork. Choose it if: relocation happens. First actions: entity + residency, then EOI at the next round.

**Path 3 – Small publisher / project fund.** After phase B evidence: pitch Outersloth (open, solo-friendly, 50/50→15%, IP kept), Griffin SOF, Hooded Horse, Yogscast Games with a $30–120k milestone ask covering T1 + a $25k marketing tier. Cost: 15–50% of revenue for the term; 2–6 months of process; entity + clean license required. Choose it if: phase B shows real wishlist velocity and you want marketing muscle + validation more than maximum ownership.

**Path 4 – Crowdfunding layer.** Post-NZ, timed to the Steam page: a $20–60k campaign as audience proof + wishlist engine, not as the budget. Choose it if: Paths 1–2 are working and you want a discovery event before 1.0.

**Path 5 – Venture scale (not recommended now).** Only if the ambition becomes a repeatable sports-sim platform (multiple sports off one engine, live-service, team) – then SPEEDRUN/pre-seed math starts to apply. Choosing this means building a different company; nothing in Parts II–III supports it today.

These compose: the realistic master plan is **1 → (2 if NZ) → 3 optionally at phase C/D** – bootstrap the evidence, let non-dilutive money extend the runway, and only take a revenue-share partner when their marketing leverage prices better than the share they take.
